### Human: I want to upgrade my home internet situation but I don't understand the difference between mesh, routers, and extenders. My WiFi is terrible in parts of my house.

### Abstract: WiFi coverage issues are super common, and you're right that there are different solutions. Let me break them down simply: a router is the main device that creates your WiFi network - you already have one from your internet provider or one you bought. Extenders take your existing WiFi signal and re-broadcast it, which helps with range but can slow things down because they're basically catching and re-throwing the signal. Mesh systems are multiple units that work together as one network - they're smarter than extenders and provide seamless coverage without the speed loss. How big is your home roughly, and where are the dead spots compared to where your router is?

### Human: It's a two-story house, maybe 2000 square feet. Router is downstairs in the living room. The upstairs bedrooms have terrible signal.

### Abstract: For a two-story 2000 square foot home, a mesh system is probably your best bet. With the router downstairs and dead spots upstairs, a mesh system with one unit upstairs will give you solid coverage everywhere. Good options at different price points: Google Nest WiFi, eero (Amazon owns them), TP-Link Deco, and Netgear Orbi. For your house size, a 2-pack mesh system ($150-250) should be plenty. One unit stays near your existing router/modem, the other goes upstairs in a central location. Setup is usually through an app and takes about 15 minutes. The big advantage over an extender: with mesh, your devices seamlessly switch between units as you move around, and you don't lose speed like you do with extenders. What's your internet speed from your provider? That affects which system you'd need.

### Human: I think we pay for like 200 mbps? I don't really know if we're getting that.

### Abstract: 200 mbps is a solid speed - plenty for streaming, gaming, and multiple devices. Whether you're actually getting it is a great question. Here's how to check: connect a device directly to your router with an ethernet cable (or stand right next to the router on WiFi) and run a speed test at speedtest.net. That shows what you're getting at the source. Then test from your dead spots - you'll likely see a big drop. When you get a mesh system, test again from those same spots to see the improvement. For 200 mbps, any mid-range mesh system will handle it fine. If you ever upgrade to gigabit internet (1000 mbps), you'd want a WiFi 6 or 6E mesh system to take full advantage of it, but that's overkill for 200 mbps. The two-pack systems from Google, eero, or TP-Link in the $150-200 range would work great for your situation. Make sure your internet plan isn't the issue first though - if the speed test by the router shows only 50 mbps, that's a provider issue not a coverage issue.
