### Human: I want to learn basic car maintenance so I stop paying for things I could do myself. What should I start with?

### Abstract: Great idea - basic car maintenance can save you hundreds of dollars a year and helps you understand your vehicle better. The good news is the easiest stuff is also the most frequent, so you'll get lots of practice. Let's start with the absolute basics that anyone can do with minimal tools. Do you have any tools at home, and are you comfortable getting a little dirty? Also, what kind of car do you have? Some cars are easier to work on than others.

### Human: I have like a basic screwdriver set, nothing fancy. I drive a Honda Civic which I've heard is pretty easy to work on.

### Abstract: Perfect - Civics are very DIY-friendly with tons of tutorials available. Start with these easy wins that need minimal tools: Oil check - locate your dipstick (yellow handle usually), check monthly, add oil if low. YouTube "Honda Civic dipstick location" if unsure. Air filter replacement - takes 5 minutes, no tools needed, just unclip the housing and swap the filter. Do this yearly, saves $30-50 in labor. Windshield wipers - just lift blade, unhook old, hook new. Takes 2 minutes. Tire pressure - get a $5 tire gauge and check monthly. There's a sticker inside your door jamb with correct pressure. Filling tires is free at most gas stations. These four things alone keep your car healthy and cost almost nothing. Once you're comfortable, the next level is oil changes and brake inspections, but those require more tools and a bit more learning.

### Human: How hard is changing oil myself? That seems like it could save a lot of money.

### Abstract: Oil changes are manageable but a bigger step up. Honest assessment: at shop prices of $40-70 for conventional or $60-100 for synthetic, you might only save $15-30 doing it yourself after buying oil, filter, and tools. The real value is knowing it's done right and learning your car. What you need: socket set, oil drain pan, funnel, jack and jack stands (don't work under a car on just the jack - dangerous), oil filter wrench, new oil and filter for your specific car. First time takes maybe an hour with YouTube guidance, faster once you've done it a few times. The process: warm up car, lift and secure it, drain old oil (messy), remove old filter, install new filter, add new oil, check level. It's straightforward but does require getting under the car. Many people decide it's not worth the hassle for the small savings, and that's valid. I'd recommend doing it at least once to understand the process, then decide if you want to continue or just pay someone.
