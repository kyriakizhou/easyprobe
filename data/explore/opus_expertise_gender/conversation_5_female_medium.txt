### Human: I've been running a small Etsy shop selling handmade jewelry for 2 years with decent success, but growth has plateaued. I'm wondering if I should expand to my own website or other platforms. What are the trade-offs?

### Assistant: Congratulations on 2 years of success! The plateau is a common growth stage. Each option has trade-offs. Etsy gives you built-in traffic but takes fees (transaction, payment processing, ads) and you're competing with millions of sellers. Your own website (Shopify, Squarespace) gives you more control and margins but requires driving your own traffic. Other marketplaces (Amazon Handmade, Faire for wholesale) offer new audiences but different fee structures and competition. What's your current profit margin and how much are you paying Etsy?

### Human: Etsy fees are eating about 15-20% between everything. My margins are okay but could be better. Most of my sales come from Etsy search - I'm not great at marketing.

### Assistant: That's a significant fee bite! Here's a practical approach: don't abandon Etsy (it's driving your sales), but start building alternatives. A simple Shopify or Squarespace site establishes your brand and captures higher-margin sales from repeat customers or people who find you through Etsy but want to order direct. Start an email list now - even just collecting emails through Etsy shop updates. When you have your own site, you can offer incentives for direct orders. Instagram is valuable for handmade - your product photographs well and has a natural audience there.

### Human: I've tried Instagram but posting consistently is hard while making products and handling orders. How do I balance everything?

### Assistant: Time management is the biggest challenge for solo makers! Some strategies: batch content creation (photograph multiple pieces in one session for weeks of posts), use a scheduler like Later or Planoly, focus on one platform rather than spreading thin, and consider templates for consistent but quick posting. You don't need daily posts - 3-4 quality posts per week with engagement is better than daily but inconsistent. Also consider what parts of your business could be streamlined or outsourced: packaging, shipping, bookkeeping? Freeing up time there gives you more for marketing.

### Human: What about wholesale? I've seen Faire mentioned but don't know much about it.

### Assistant: Faire is interesting for expanding reach - it's a wholesale marketplace where retailers discover makers. You sell at wholesale prices (typically 50% of retail) but in larger quantities. Pros: predictable larger orders, exposure to brick-and-mortar stores, diversified revenue. Cons: lower per-unit profit, minimum order requirements, possible delays in payment. It works best if your production can scale and your retail margins are healthy enough to absorb the wholesale cut. Before jumping in, make sure your costs allow for sustainable wholesale pricing. Some makers do both retail and wholesale successfully with slightly different product lines.

### Human: This is helpful. If you had to prioritize, what should I focus on first?

### Assistant: Priority order: 1) Start collecting emails immediately (this costs nothing and builds your most valuable asset), 2) Set up a simple website with your best sellers (even basic - just establish the presence), 3) Batch-create content for Instagram and commit to consistent posting for 3 months to see results, 4) Consider wholesale only after you've stabilized your direct sales channels. Don't try everything at once - that's how you burn out. Pick one new initiative per quarter and execute it well before adding another. Your Etsy foundation is solid; you're building on it, not replacing it.
