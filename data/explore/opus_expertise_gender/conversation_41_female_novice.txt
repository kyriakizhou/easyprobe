### Human: Hello! I've been having trouble sleeping and I've heard about "sleep hygiene" but I don't really know what that means. Can you explain?

### Assistant: Of course! "Sleep hygiene" is just a term for healthy habits and practices that help you sleep better. It's not about cleanliness - think of it as taking care of your sleep the way you might take care of your body through exercise or diet. What does your typical evening and sleep situation look like right now?

### Human: I usually watch TV in bed until I fall asleep, scroll my phone if I wake up at night, and I drink coffee throughout the day. I'm exhausted but can't seem to sleep well.

### Assistant: Those are actually some of the most common sleep disruptors! Let me explain why. Screens (TV, phone) emit blue light that tricks your brain into thinking it's daytime, suppressing the sleep hormone melatonin. Doing activities other than sleep in bed weakens the mental association between bed and sleep. And caffeine stays in your system for hours - coffee after noon can still affect you at bedtime. These are all fixable with some habit changes!

### Human: What time should I stop drinking coffee?

### Assistant: A good rule is to stop caffeine by 2pm, or even noon if you're sensitive to it. Caffeine has a "half-life" of about 5-6 hours, meaning half of it is still in your system after that time. So afternoon coffee can definitely interfere with sleep at night. Try switching to decaf or herbal tea after lunch. If you rely on afternoon coffee to stay awake, that itself might be a sign you need better sleep - it can become a vicious cycle!

### Human: What about the TV and phone thing? I feel like I need them to wind down.

### Assistant: I understand - screens feel relaxing in the moment! But they're actually stimulating your brain, not calming it. Try creating a "power down hour" before bed: no screens for at least 30-60 minutes before sleep. Replace them with truly relaxing activities: reading a physical book, gentle stretching, taking a warm bath, or listening to calm music or a podcast. It feels weird at first, but your brain will start associating these activities with winding down. Keep your phone charging outside the bedroom so you're not tempted at night.

### Human: What else helps? I really need to sleep better.

### Assistant: A few more tips: keep a consistent sleep schedule (same bedtime and wake time, even weekends), make your room cool, dark, and quiet, avoid eating large meals close to bedtime, and limit alcohol (it disrupts sleep quality even if it helps you fall asleep). Regular exercise helps sleep, but not too close to bedtime. If you can't fall asleep after 20 minutes, get up and do something quiet until you feel sleepy - lying there frustrated makes it worse. These changes take time to show effects, so be patient!
