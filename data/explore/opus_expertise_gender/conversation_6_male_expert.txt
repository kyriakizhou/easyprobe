### Human: I'm the CMO at a B2B SaaS company with about $50M ARR. We're trying to build a more sophisticated attribution model to understand what's actually driving pipeline, but the complexity of B2B buyer journeys makes this incredibly difficult. What approaches have you seen work?

### Assistant: B2B attribution is genuinely hard because the buying journey is non-linear, involves multiple stakeholders, and spans months or quarters. The "right" touch is often impossible to isolate because deals require multiple touchpoints working together. Common approaches and their limitations: first-touch attribution: credits the channel that first brought someone into your funnel. Useful for understanding awareness drivers but ignores everything that happens after. In B2B, first touch might be 18 months before close. Last-touch attribution: credits whatever happened before the deal closed. Often credits sales activity or direct response while ignoring marketing that built awareness and consideration. Linear/time-decay models: distribute credit across touchpoints. Better than single-touch but still arbitrary - a webinar and a trade show booth visit aren't necessarily equal contributions. Multi-touch attribution (MTA): sophisticated statistical models that try to determine incremental contribution of each channel. Can work at scale with good data, but requires significant data volume and still struggles with correlation-causation issues in B2B. What actually works for companies at your scale and complexity: recognize attribution is directional, not precise. You're trying to inform investment decisions, not achieve accounting accuracy. Hybrid approaches combining quantitative signals with qualitative insight tend to be most useful. What does your current data infrastructure look like - CRM, marketing automation, and can you track touchpoints across the full buyer journey?

### Human: We're on Salesforce and HubSpot with reasonably clean integration. We can track most digital touchpoints but struggle with things like word-of-mouth, dark social, and what happens in accounts we're not tracking before they enter our funnel. Attribution always seems to favor the channels we can measure.

### Abstract: You've identified the fundamental problem - attribution is biased toward measurable channels. This systematically undervalues brand, word-of-mouth, podcasts, communities, and the vast majority of awareness-building that happens before someone hits your website. Approaches to address this: self-reported attribution: add "How did you hear about us?" to your demo request or signup forms. Make it open-ended or include options for dark funnel sources. This is subjective but captures signal you can't track. I've seen this reveal that 30-40% of pipeline credits channels that wouldn't show up in multi-touch attribution. Compare self-reported to system attribution to understand your measurement blind spots. Account-level journey mapping: for your top 20-30 won deals each quarter, do qualitative interviews with the buyer and the sales team. Map the actual decision process - what drove initial awareness, what built credibility, what overcame objections. This won't give you statistical power but will surface patterns that attribution models miss. Incrementality testing: rather than modeling attribution, test the incremental impact of channels directly. Run holdout tests - stop spending in a region or segment and measure impact on pipeline. This is harder in B2B with small numbers and long cycles, but geographic or account-segment holdouts can work. Media mix modeling: statistical approach that correlates marketing spend with outcomes at an aggregate level, accounting for lag effects and saturation. More common in B2C but applicable for B2B at your scale. Requires at least 2-3 years of data and sufficient spend variation to detect signal. Investment framework implication: instead of trying to attribute every dollar, think about your marketing mix as a portfolio. Some investments (performance marketing) are measurable and optimizable. Others (brand, content, community) are strategic bets evaluated on longer-term metrics like aided awareness, share of voice, and qualitative pipeline quality signals.

### Human: The self-reported attribution approach is interesting - we've actually tried this but got a lot of "Google search" responses which doesn't tell us much. How do you get more useful signal from self-reported data?

### Abstract: "Google search" is the default answer when the real answer requires more thought. The form design and question framing significantly impact response quality. Improving self-reported attribution: question design: instead of "How did you hear about us?" try "What first made you aware of [Company]?" or "Before searching for us, what prompted you to look for a solution like ours?" These questions get at the upstream source before the Google search. Multi-question approach: ask two questions: "What prompted you to look for this type of solution?" (captures problem awareness trigger) and "Where did you first learn about [Company] specifically?" (captures brand discovery). The combination is more informative than either alone. Specific option framing: if you use multiple choice, make dark funnel options prominent and specific. Include: "Recommendation from colleague/peer," "Podcast (which one?)," "Newsletter (which one?)," "Industry community or forum," "Saw someone mention us on LinkedIn." The specificity signals that these are valid answers and jogs memory. Follow-up mechanism: for high-value leads, have SDRs ask attribution questions in discovery calls. "What's been driving your evaluation of solutions like ours? How'd we end up on your list?" Conversational context gets better answers than form fields. Qualitative deep-dives: survey recent closed-won customers (not just at form submission). Ask them to walk through their buyer journey retrospectively. Record and analyze these conversations. Ten detailed buyer journey interviews will tell you more than thousands of "Google search" form responses. CRM enrichment: train sales team to capture attribution signal in call notes. Create a structured field for "attribution notes" that captures qualitative detail that doesn't fit dropdown menus. Periodically analyze these notes for patterns. Triangulation: combine self-reported data with other signals. If someone says "Google search" but they've been opening your newsletter for 6 months, the newsletter likely built the awareness that the search captured. Look for these patterns at scale.
