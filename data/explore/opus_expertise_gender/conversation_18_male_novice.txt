### Human: I need to buy a suit for a job interview but I've never bought one before. I usually just wear jeans. What do I need to know?

### Abstract: Getting your first suit is a milestone! The good news is you don't need to spend a fortune to look professional. First question: what industry are you interviewing in? Finance, law, and consulting typically expect traditional suits, while tech or creative fields might be fine with business casual. Also, what's your budget? There are decent options at various price points.

### Human: It's for a corporate finance position. Budget is maybe $300-400? I want to look professional but I can't spend a ton.

### Abstract: For corporate finance at that budget, you can definitely look professional. Go for a solid navy or charcoal gray suit - these are classic, versatile, and appropriate for almost any business setting. Avoid black (actually looks more formal/funeral-like) or trendy colors for your first suit. For $300-400, check out stores like Macy's (Bar III or Alfani brands), J.Crew Factory, Banana Republic (on sale), or Suit Supply if you can stretch slightly. The most important thing is fit - a $200 suit that fits well looks better than a $500 suit that doesn't. Get it tailored if needed (usually $30-75 for basic adjustments). The suit should button without pulling, shoulders should hit right at your shoulder, and sleeves should show about half inch of shirt cuff.

### Human: What else do I need besides the suit? Shirt, shoes, tie?

### Abstract: Yes, you'll need a few more pieces but it's not complicated. Shirt: white or light blue dress shirt - crisp, classic, safe choice. Make sure the collar fits (shouldn't gap or choke). Budget $30-50. Tie: solid color or simple pattern, not too flashy. Navy, burgundy, or blue are safe bets. Match it to your suit - navy suit with burgundy tie works great. $20-40. Shoes: black or dark brown leather dress shoes, simple style like oxfords or derbies. Make sure they're polished. $60-100 will get you something decent. Belt: should match your shoes - black shoes mean black belt, brown shoes mean brown belt. $20-30. Socks: dress socks that match your pants, no white athletic socks! $5-10. Total you're looking at maybe $450-550 for everything, but these pieces will serve you well beyond just this interview. Get the suit first and make sure it fits before buying everything else.
