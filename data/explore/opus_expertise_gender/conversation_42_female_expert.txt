### Human: I'm structuring a secondary transaction for a GP-led continuation fund. Our LP base has mixed interests - some want liquidity, others want to continue with the assets. The process design and pricing mechanics are complex. How do you think about fairness and conflicts in these transactions?

### Assistant: GP-led continuation funds are inherently conflicted transactions - the GP is on both sides, selling assets to a vehicle they'll continue to manage. This creates pricing tension (GP benefits from both realized carry on the sale and future carry on the continuation), LP treatment disparities, and information asymmetries. The fairness framework needs to address: pricing validation (is the transaction price fair relative to alternatives?), LP optionality (can LPs choose to exit or continue without coercion?), conflict mitigation (how are GP conflicts disclosed and managed?), and process integrity (is there independent oversight?). What's your LP composition - are there large institutional LPs with governance rights, or is it more fragmented?

### Human: Mix of large institutions (about 40% of commitments) and smaller LPs including fund-of-funds and family offices. The large institutions have LPAC seats and are engaged; smaller LPs are more passive. The institutions are pushing back on process and pricing.

### Assistant: Institutional pushback on process and pricing is appropriate and expected - they understand the conflicts and have governance mechanisms to exercise. The challenge is ensuring smaller, passive LPs get equivalent protection without the same engagement capacity. Process design for fairness: independent LPAC oversight of the transaction, with institutions representing LP interests in process design and pricing negotiation. Third-party valuation from an advisor not conflicted by relationship with GP. Market check - either a formal process that tests whether the continuation fund offer is competitive, or LPAC sign-off that waiving market check is appropriate given circumstances. Clear disclosure of GP economics under different scenarios (current sale, continuation fund, hold to maturity) so LPs can evaluate GP incentives. LP optionality: each LP should have genuine choice to take liquidity at the transaction price, roll into continuation vehicle, or some combination. No coercion through information withholding or pressure tactics. Identical terms for all LPs regardless of engagement level. Pricing mechanics: for institutions pushing back on price, the question is whether their objection is to process (they want more validation) or to outcome (they think price is wrong). Which is it?

### Human: Primarily process - they want a more robust market check before accepting the continuation fund price. They're concerned we're setting a price that benefits the GP's dual position rather than maximizing LP value.

### Assistant: Institutional concern about market check is well-founded and should be accommodated. Options for market validation: full sale process - run a competitive auction for the assets with continuation fund as one bidder. This provides definitive market price but takes time, creates execution risk, and may not be appropriate if the thesis is specifically about GP continuation value. Stapled secondary process - invite secondary buyers to bid on LP interests at various prices, establishing market clearing price for liquidity seekers. GP continuation offer should match or exceed this. Third-party bid - solicit at least one credible third-party offer (another GP, strategic buyer) that establishes market alternative even if not a full auction. This provides pricing reference without full process. Fairness opinion - independent financial advisor opines on price fairness, considering comparable transactions, valuation methodologies, and alternatives. Weaker than market check but provides process protection. For your situation: if institutions are demanding market check, provide one. The GP's credibility and LP relationships are worth more than any pricing optimization from avoiding market validation. A process that institutions can't criticize protects the GP even if it results in slightly lower pricing. What's the GP's objection to running a market check - is it timing, execution risk, or concern about what the market will reveal?

### Human: GP is concerned that a full process will take too long and create uncertainty that could affect portfolio company performance. They also believe the current pricing reflects fair value and a market check will just confirm that while adding cost and delay.

### Assistant: GP concerns about timing and disruption are legitimate but can be managed, and the "market check will just confirm fair value" argument actually supports doing it - if GP is confident in pricing, market validation removes LP objections at modest cost. Process design to address GP concerns: compressed timeline - secondary market processes can run in 6-8 weeks with good preparation. Coordinate with institutions on acceptable timeline that balances thoroughness with efficiency. Controlled information flow - structure the process to minimize portfolio company exposure. Secondary buyers evaluate LP interests based on GP-provided information, not direct portfolio company diligence that creates disruption. Parallel paths - begin continuation fund documentation while market check runs, so execution can be fast once pricing is validated. Cost allocation - transaction costs are typically borne by the fund; GP shouldn't bear disproportionate cost for process that protects LP interests. The institutional pushback reality: if 40% of your LP base is demanding more robust process and you refuse, you risk either transaction failure or damaged relationships that affect future fundraising. The cost of accommodating their concerns is almost certainly lower than the cost of conflict. Recommended approach: propose a streamlined market check process with defined timeline and scope that addresses institutional concerns without creating extended uncertainty. Position it as GP demonstrating commitment to fair process, not GP capitulating to LP demands. What's the timeline pressure driving the transaction - is there a specific reason it needs to close by a certain date?
