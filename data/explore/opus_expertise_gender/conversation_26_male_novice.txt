### Human: I need to do my own taxes for the first time. I've always had my parents help but I moved out and should probably learn. Where do I start?

### Abstract: Doing your own taxes sounds intimidating but for most people with straightforward situations, it's actually pretty manageable. First, some questions to understand your situation: do you just have W-2 income from a regular job, or do you have other income sources like freelancing, investments, or rental property? Do you own a home, or rent? Any student loans or major life changes? The simpler your situation, the easier your taxes.

### Human: Just W-2 income from my job. I rent, have student loans that I'm paying off, and I have a basic savings account. That's pretty much it.

### Abstract: Perfect - that's about as simple as taxes get! You can absolutely do this yourself. Here's what to do: wait for your W-2 form from your employer (should arrive by end of January), and your 1098-E form from your student loan servicer showing interest you paid. Those are probably your only tax documents. Use free tax software - IRS Free File if your income is under $84,000, or FreeTaxUSA (very cheap, excellent). TurboTax and H&R Block work but are pricier. The software walks you through everything step by step like a questionnaire. Enter your info from the W-2, answer questions about deductions (student loan interest is one you'll claim), and the software calculates what you owe or get back. Most people in your situation just take the standard deduction and it takes maybe an hour. File electronically and you'll get any refund in 1-3 weeks via direct deposit.

### Human: What's a W-2 versus a 1099? I've heard both terms.

### Abstract: Good question - they're different types of tax forms for different types of income. W-2: you get this from an employer where you're a regular employee. They withhold taxes from each paycheck, pay half your Social Security/Medicare taxes, and handle a lot of the tax complexity for you. Most common for people with "normal" jobs. 1099: you get various types of 1099s for other income. 1099-NEC or 1099-MISC means you did freelance or contract work - no taxes withheld, you owe all of it yourself, including self-employment tax. More complicated. 1099-INT means you earned interest (your savings account, though probably tiny). 1099-DIV is for investment dividends. If you only have W-2 income, your taxes are much simpler. Your employer already sent money to the IRS on your behalf throughout the year - your tax return just calculates whether they sent too much (you get a refund) or too little (you owe more). Sounds like you're W-2 only, so you're in good shape.
