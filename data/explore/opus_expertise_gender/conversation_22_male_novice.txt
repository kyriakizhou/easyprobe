### Human: I need to give a best man speech at my friend's wedding and I'm terrified. I've never spoken in front of a crowd. Help?

### Abstract: Best man speeches feel scary, but here's the good news: the crowd is on your side. Everyone wants you to do well, and it's a celebration, not a judgment. Wedding speeches are also pretty forgiving - even imperfect ones get warm receptions because people are happy. First question: when's the wedding? Knowing the timeline helps me suggest how to prepare. And how well do you know the bride - is she a long-time friend too, or mostly someone you know through the groom?

### Human: Wedding is in about 6 weeks. I know the bride decently well - they've been together for 4 years and we've hung out as couples quite a bit. I have no idea what to even say though.

### Abstract: Six weeks is plenty of time! Here's a simple structure that works: start with a brief intro of who you are and how you know the groom (30 seconds). Share one or two short, appropriate stories that show his character or your friendship - funny is great but keep it wedding-appropriate, no embarrassing exes or wild party stories (1-2 minutes). Talk about when he met the bride and how you've seen him become happier or better because of her (1 minute). End with a sincere toast to their future together and raise your glass (30 seconds). Total: 3-4 minutes max. People prefer short and sweet to long and rambling. Think about: a funny first impression you had of him, a moment that showed his loyalty or character, and how he talks about her or acts around her. Start jotting notes now even if they're messy.

### Human: Should I memorize the whole thing? I'm worried about freezing up.

### Abstract: Don't fully memorize word-for-word - that's actually when people freeze up, because if you lose your place, you're lost. Instead, memorize your key points and transitions. Know your opening line cold (that helps you start confidently), know your stories so well you could tell them naturally, and know your closing toast word-for-word (for a strong finish). Bring note cards or your phone with bullet points as backup - totally acceptable and nobody will judge you. Practice out loud at least 5-6 times, ideally to a friend or family member for feedback. Record yourself to catch any nervous habits. On the day: have one drink for courage if you want, but not more. Take a breath before you start. Speak slowly - nerves make you rush. If you stumble, just pause and continue. The crowd is rooting for you. You've got six weeks; you'll be more prepared than you think.
