### Assistant: At 35 with $150k income, your 90/10 allocation is aggressive but reasonable given your timeline. However, I see a few optimization opportunities before diversifying into new vehicles: You're likely eligible for a backdoor Roth IRA even at your income level - this gives you tax-free growth for retirement, which is valuable diversification of your tax situation, not just asset allocation. If you have HDHP access, the HSA is arguably the most tax-advantaged account that exists - triple tax benefit (deduction, growth, and withdrawal if used for medical). At your income, these tax diversification moves probably add more value than changing your asset allocation. That said, your 100% US equity exposure is geographically concentrated. Adding 20-30% international developed and emerging markets exposure would reduce country-specific risk while maintaining stock exposure. What's your current thinking on real estate or bonds?

### Human: I've thought about real estate but owning rental property seems like a lot of hassle. Is it worth it?

### Abstract: Direct rental property ownership is definitely more work than passive index investing - you're essentially starting a small business with tenant management, maintenance, vacancies, and potential liability. The returns can be excellent due to leverage and tax advantages, but the "hassle" is real and the returns aren't guaranteed. For someone who doesn't want to be a landlord, there are alternatives: REITs (real estate investment trusts) give you real estate exposure with the liquidity and passivity of stocks. You can add REIT index funds to your portfolio for maybe 5-10% allocation. Private real estate crowdfunding platforms like Fundrise or CrowdStreet offer exposure to commercial real estate deals, though they're less liquid and have higher minimums. Or you could look at real estate debt funds. My honest take: at 35 with a solid career, if you're not excited about being a landlord, don't force it. REITs plus your existing equity allocation gives you plenty of growth potential. The "rich landlord" path works great for people who enjoy it, but there are other ways to build wealth.
