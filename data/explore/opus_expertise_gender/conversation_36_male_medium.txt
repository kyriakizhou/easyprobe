### Human: I've been a hobbyist 3D printer for about 2 years, printing mostly functional parts and some models. I'm getting interested in resin printing but I don't know if the additional complexity is worth it. What should I consider?

### Assistant: Resin (SLA/MSLA) printing is a different beast from FDM - not necessarily harder, but different tradeoffs. The quality difference is dramatic: resin produces parts with detail and surface finish that FDM can't match. But the workflow involves handling liquid resin, post-processing (washing and curing), and dealing with toxic materials. For functional parts that don't need extreme detail, FDM is often still better (stronger, cheaper per part, less hassle). Resin excels at miniatures, jewelry, dental/medical models, and anything requiring fine detail or smooth surfaces. What's driving your interest - specific projects that need better detail, or general curiosity?

### Human: I've been wanting to print miniatures for tabletop gaming. FDM minis always look layer-y no matter what I do. I know resin is better for that but I'm worried about the safety and mess factor.

### Abstract: Miniatures are the perfect use case for resin - you'll be amazed at the quality difference compared to FDM. Modern MSLA printers produce minis that look commercially molded. The safety and mess concerns are legitimate but manageable with proper setup: safety reality check: uncured resin is a skin sensitizer (can cause allergic reactions with repeated exposure), should not be ingested, and produces fumes. You need nitrile gloves always, eye protection, and good ventilation. The workspace needs to be somewhere you can contain spills and keep away from children/pets. It's not scary-dangerous, but it requires respect and consistent habits. Mess factor: you will get resin on things. It's sticky and annoying to clean up until cured. Dedicated workspace with disposable coverings helps. Post-processing means washing parts in IPA or a cleaning solution, then UV curing. This adds 10-15 minutes per print versus FDM's remove-and-done workflow. Smell: some resins smell more than others. Eco-friendly/plant-based resins and water-washable resins reduce this and simplify cleanup, though they have their own tradeoffs. For miniatures specifically: you'll want to learn about supports and orientation to avoid scarring visible surfaces. It's a learning curve but there are great community resources.

### Human: What printer would you recommend for getting started with miniatures? I don't want to spend a fortune since I don't know if I'll stick with it.

### Abstract: For miniature printing specifically, you don't need to spend much. The entry-level MSLA market is extremely competitive and even budget printers produce excellent minis. Good starter options: Elegoo Mars 4 or Anycubic Photon Mono 2 - both around $200-250, large communities, good documentation, and produce minis indistinguishable from more expensive machines. The print quality differences at this level are negligible for miniatures. The Saturn 3 (Elegoo) or similar larger format is worth considering if you want to print multiple minis at once or larger pieces, but it costs more and takes up more space. Beyond the printer: budget $50-100 for essential accessories you'll need immediately: wash and cure station (or DIY containers plus a UV lamp), IPA or water-washable resin, nitrile gloves, cleaning supplies, and a basic tool kit for support removal. Total starting investment: $300-400 gets you printing quality minis. Compare that to the hundreds you'd spend on miniature kits or commissions. My suggestion: get an entry-level printer (Mars 4 or equivalent), a bottle of gray resin (easiest to see detail), and start with a test model. Don't over-invest in accessories until you know you'll stick with it. Many people buy elaborate wash/cure stations before printing their first model - unnecessary at the start.
