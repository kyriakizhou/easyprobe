### Human: I need to learn Excel for work but I'm really bad at computers. Where do I even start?

### Abstract: Excel can feel overwhelming but the basics are actually pretty intuitive once you get the hang of it. Most people only ever use maybe 20% of Excel's features anyway, and that 20% isn't complicated. Let me understand what you're dealing with: what kind of work do you need Excel for - is it simple data entry and organization, calculations and formulas, making charts, or something more complex? And when you say you're "bad at computers," do you mean you're not tech-savvy in general, or specifically that spreadsheets confuse you?

### Human: Mainly tracking numbers and doing some basic math - budgets, expenses, that kind of thing. I just don't know how to do anything beyond type in cells. Spreadsheets specifically confuse me.

### Abstract: For budgets and expense tracking, you only need a few core skills. Let's break it down: First, understand that Excel is just a grid where each box (cell) has an address like A1, B2, etc. Column letters run across the top, row numbers down the side. That's the foundation. For basic math, you use formulas that start with =. Type =A1+A2 and it adds those cells. Type =SUM(A1:A10) and it adds everything from A1 to A10. This is how you total columns of numbers. Other common formulas: =AVERAGE, =COUNT, =MAX, =MIN. For budgets, you'd typically put categories in one column, amounts in the next, and a SUM formula at the bottom to total it. The best way to learn is by doing. YouTube has tons of beginner tutorials - search "Excel absolute beginners" and follow along with a practice file. Microsoft also has free Excel tutorials that are pretty good.

### Human: Those formulas sound confusing. Is there an easier way to learn?

### Abstract: Totally understandable - formulas look intimidating as text but make more sense when you actually do them. Here's the easiest path: don't start with written tutorials. Go to YouTube and search "Excel for absolute beginners 2024" - watch a video where someone shares their screen and you follow along step by step. Pause, try it yourself, rewind if needed. Watching someone do it makes it click much faster than reading instructions. Start with just one thing: learn to SUM a column of numbers. Type some numbers in cells A1 through A5, then in A6 type =SUM(A1:A5) and press Enter. Watch it add up. Play with changing the numbers and watch the total update automatically. That one formula handles most of what you need for basic budgets. There's also an easy shortcut: if you highlight a bunch of cells, Excel shows the sum at the bottom of your screen automatically. For now, focus on just that one skill until it feels natural, then add the next one. Baby steps.
