### Human: I want to plan a trip abroad but I've never traveled internationally before. I don't even have a passport. What do I need to do?

### Abstract: Exciting! International travel is amazing and more accessible than people think. First things first: get that passport. It's the most important step and takes time, so start now regardless of when you're planning to travel. Apply at a passport acceptance facility (usually post offices or government offices), bring your birth certificate, a passport photo, and pay the fee (currently $165 for a new adult passport). Standard processing is 6-8 weeks; pay extra for expedited if you need it faster. While that processes, you can start planning everything else. Where are you thinking of going?

### Human: I'm not sure yet. Maybe Europe because it seems safe and easy for first-time travelers. Budget isn't huge - maybe $2,000-3,000 total for like a week?

### Abstract: Europe is a great first international trip - lots of English speakers, good infrastructure, and amazing variety. For $2,000-3,000 for a week, your destination matters a lot. Western Europe (London, Paris, Amsterdam) is expensive; Eastern and Southern Europe (Portugal, Spain, Czech Republic, Croatia, Greece) are much more affordable. My suggestion for a first-timer on that budget: pick one city or region, don't try to see everything. Portugal (Lisbon) is beautiful, affordable, and very tourist-friendly. Barcelona, Prague, and Budapest are also great first destinations. Budget breakdown: flights are often the biggest cost ($600-1200 round trip depending on timing and destination), accommodations ($50-150/night), food ($30-60/day), activities and transportation. Book flights 2-3 months ahead for best prices; use Google Flights to track prices. Consider shoulder season (April-May or September-October) for lower prices and fewer crowds.

### Human: What about actually getting around and not getting lost? I don't speak any other languages.

### Abstract: Getting around is so much easier now than it used to be, and language is rarely a barrier in tourist areas. Download Google Maps offline for your destination - this is essential. Even without cell service, you can navigate anywhere. For data abroad, either get an international plan from your carrier (usually $10/day) or buy a local SIM card when you arrive (much cheaper). Most major European cities have great public transit - metros and buses that are easy to figure out. Google Maps tells you exactly which trains to take. English is widely spoken in tourist areas, hotels, restaurants, and airports throughout Europe. Learn a few basic words in the local language (hello, thank you, excuse me) - locals really appreciate the effort. Restaurant menus often have English or pictures. Honestly, millions of Americans travel to Europe every year without speaking the language and do just fine. The biggest tip: have your accommodation address written down in case you need to show a taxi driver.
