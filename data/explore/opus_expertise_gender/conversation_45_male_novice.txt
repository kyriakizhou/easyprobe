### Human: I want to start a side business or hustle but I have no special skills. What can someone with no experience actually do?

### Abstract: First, I'd challenge the idea that you have "no special skills" - most people underestimate what they know or can do. Skills you use at work, hobbies, life experiences, even being organized or reliable are valuable. That said, there are legitimate side hustles that don't require specialized skills to start. Let me ask: how much time can you realistically commit weekly? Do you prefer something flexible or structured? And are you looking for something online or in-person?

### Human: Maybe 10-15 hours a week after my regular job. Flexible would be better. Either online or in-person is fine.

### Abstract: For 10-15 hours with flexibility, here are realistic options: service-based (in-person): dog walking/pet sitting (Rover, Wag), lawn care and basic yard work, cleaning services, moving help (TaskRabbit, Dolly). These require hustle but no special skills - you learn on the job and build from there. Gig economy: DoorDash, Uber, Instacart - very flexible but the per-hour rate varies a lot and depends on your market. Online: freelance virtual assistant work (scheduling, email, data entry), reselling (buy low at thrift stores, sell higher on eBay/Poshmark - requires learning what sells), or user testing for websites (UserTesting.com pays for your feedback on sites). Avoid: anything that asks you to pay money upfront, MLMs, or "get rich quick" schemes. If it sounds too good to be true, it is. The unsexy truth is that most side hustles require trading time for money initially. The path to passive income or scalable business comes later after you've built skills and capital.

### Human: How do I know which one to actually try?

### Abstract: Start by considering: what do you have already? A car opens up delivery/rideshare. A computer with internet opens online options. Good physical fitness makes moving help or lawn care easier. Then think about what you'd tolerate doing for money - not necessarily love, but at least not hate. If you dislike driving, skip DoorDash. If you don't like dogs, skip pet sitting. My advice: pick one thing and commit to trying it for 30 days. Don't spend weeks researching the "perfect" side hustle - you'll learn more from actually doing one than from reading about all of them. The first one might not stick, and that's fine. Many successful side businesses came after trying and abandoning other ideas. Keep startup costs near zero for your first attempt. If lawn care interests you, start with a friend's lawn. If you're curious about reselling, go to a thrift store with $20 and see what you can flip. Test before investing.
