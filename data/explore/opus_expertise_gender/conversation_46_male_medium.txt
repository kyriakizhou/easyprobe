The main paths: Solutions Architect (foundational to professional track), Developer, or SysOps Administrator for associate level, then specialty certs or Professional level above that. The question is whether you want to validate your current skills or stretch into new territory. ### Human: Mix of all three - I want to validate what I know, but also push myself to learn services I haven't used. Career-wise, I'd like to move toward more senior architecture roles eventually. Which certification path makes sense?

### Abstract: For architecture track, the obvious path is Solutions Architect. Given your experience level and goals: start with Solutions Architect Associate (SAA-C03): this validates your current knowledge while filling gaps. The exam covers services you've used (EC2, RDS, S3, Lambda) plus services you might not have touched extensively (VPC networking, IAM deep dive, various database options, caching, CDN, monitoring). It's broad rather than deep. For someone with your experience, the exam is achievable with 4-8 weeks of focused study. What you'll learn beyond what you know: the exam forces you to understand service selection tradeoffs - when to use Aurora versus RDS, when Lambda versus ECS, how to design for high availability and fault tolerance. This architectural thinking is exactly what you need for senior roles. After SAA: Solutions Architect Professional is significantly harder and covers complex multi-account architectures, migrations, and optimization scenarios. That's your longer-term goal if you want to be a senior architect. Some people also do a specialty cert (Security, Networking, or Data Analytics) depending on their focus area. Study approach: don't just watch videos - use the free tier and actually build things. The hands-on experience combined with exam study creates real understanding, not just exam-passing knowledge. ### Human: I'd like to get SAA done within the next 3 months. What resources are actually good for studying? There seem to be dozens of options.

### Abstract: Three months is plenty for SAA with your background. The resource landscape is crowded but there are clear standouts: video courses: Adrian Cantrill's course is widely considered the best - thorough, well-explained, with hands-on labs. It's more expensive than some alternatives but significantly better. Stephane Maarek's Udemy course is also excellent and more affordable (always on sale for ~$15). Either works; pick based on learning style preference. Practice exams: Tutorial Dojo practice tests (Jon Bonso) are essential. The actual exam questions feel similar to these. Do all of them multiple times. Reviewing wrong answers and understanding why is where the learning happens. Don't just aim to pass - aim to understand. Official resources: AWS Skill Builder has free content including some practice questions. The AWS whitepapers (especially Well-Architected Framework) are dense but valuable for understanding AWS's recommended approaches. Hands-on: this is non-negotiable. Build a multi-tier application from scratch in the free tier. Set up VPC from scratch with public and private subnets. Deploy using different compute options. The muscle memory matters. Study approach: video course for concepts, then practice exams to identify gaps, then targeted review of weak areas, then more practice exams. Schedule the exam before you feel 100% ready - having a deadline motivates focused study. Aim for 80%+ on practice exams before sitting the real thing.
