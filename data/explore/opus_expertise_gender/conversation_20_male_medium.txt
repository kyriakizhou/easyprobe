Pricing approaches for this market: per finished minute is common for creator content - $25-75 per finished minute for intermediate-level editing with motion graphics, sound design, and color work is typical. Lower for simple cuts-only editing, higher for complex VFX or animation. Per video flat rate works once you've done enough projects to estimate accurately - "a 10-minute YouTube video with these specifications is $400." Clients like cost certainty. Hourly rate ($25-50/hour for intermediate-level work in the creator market) is simpler but clients may worry about hours creeping up, and you're not rewarded for efficiency. For starting out, I'd recommend per-minute pricing because it's easy for creators to understand and budget for. Start at the lower end of market ($25-35/minute) to build a client base and portfolio, then raise rates as you get busier. Track your actual time - if you're making less than $25/hour, you need to raise rates or get more efficient. ### Abstract: Full treatment editing for YouTube is legitimately time-intensive and should be priced accordingly. The "look like bigger YouTubers" expectation is common - creators see the finished product without understanding the work behind it. Some framing for these conversations: bigger YouTubers either have dedicated editors on salary or pay significant rates. A 15-minute well-edited video with motion graphics, sound design, custom transitions, and color might represent 20-30+ hours of work at professional speed. That's $500-1500+ for quality work. For your skill level and market: $35-50 per finished minute is reasonable for full-treatment YouTube editing with motion graphics and sound design. A 15-minute video at $40/min is $600. Frame it to clients this way - they need to understand the scope. Managing expectations is crucial: create a clear scope document. "This price includes X rounds of revisions, motion graphics for intro/outro plus X lower thirds, color correction, sound design." Add-ons like custom animations or extensive revisions are extra. This prevents scope creep. Some clients will balk at the price. That's okay - they're not your clients. Others will happily pay for quality work that makes their channel look professional.
