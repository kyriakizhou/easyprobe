### Human: I'm designing the pricing architecture for a B2B SaaS platform that serves both SMB and enterprise customers. The current one-size-fits-all model is leaving money on the table with enterprise customers and creating friction with SMBs. How should I think about segmented pricing without creating operational complexity?

### Assistant: Segmented B2B SaaS pricing requires balancing value capture across segments against operational complexity and customer experience. The core insight: SMB and enterprise customers have fundamentally different buying processes, value perception, and price sensitivity. Forcing them through the same pricing model optimizes for neither. Common architecture: self-serve pricing for SMB with transparent, published tiers based on usage or feature bundles; sales-assisted pricing for enterprise with negotiated contracts, custom terms, and value-based pricing. The boundary between segments is where complexity lives - you need clear criteria for which customers go through which motion, and handling customers who straddle the boundary. What's your current pricing model, and where specifically are you seeing money left on table with enterprise versus friction with SMB?

### Human: Current model is per-seat pricing with three tiers (Basic, Pro, Enterprise). SMBs balk at seat pricing because utilization is uneven - they're paying for seats that aren't active. Enterprise customers sign our top tier without negotiation which suggests we're underpriced, and they want customization we can't provide in standard tiers.

### Assistant: Per-seat pricing with uneven utilization is classic SMB friction - they feel penalized for organizational structure rather than paying for value received. Enterprise signing without negotiation strongly suggests underpricing at the top tier - enterprise procurement expects to negotiate, and if they're not pushing back, you're likely 30-50% below their willingness to pay. Architecture redesign: SMB model - consider moving from per-seat to usage-based or hybrid pricing. Options include active user pricing (only pay for users who actually engage), usage-based (transactions, API calls, data volume), or tiered bundles with seat ranges rather than per-seat. This aligns cost with value perception and reduces the "paying for unused seats" objection. Enterprise model - move to value-based pricing with custom contracts. Start with a higher published Enterprise tier price (anchor high), then negotiate based on scope, commitment, and strategic value. Include customization options (dedicated support, custom integrations, SLAs, professional services) as value-adds that justify higher pricing. What's your average contract value for enterprise customers currently, and what customizations are they requesting?

### Human: Enterprise ACV is around $150K currently. ### Assistant: Including SSO, advanced security, custom integrations, dedicated CS, and custom reporting in base Enterprise tier without incremental pricing is significant value leakage. Each of these is a standard enterprise upsell category. Repricing approach: redefine your Enterprise tier as the starting point, not the ceiling. Base Enterprise: core platform with SSO and standard security (these are increasingly table stakes) at higher price point than current. Enterprise Plus or custom tier: adds dedicated CSM, custom integrations, advanced reporting, enhanced SLAs - priced at 50-100% premium over base. Strategic tier: largest customers with full customization, executive engagement, and custom pricing based on value. This structure captures more value from customers already willing to pay more while creating upsell path for growing accounts. For the specific items: SSO - keep in base Enterprise, it's expected; custom integrations - should be separately scoped and priced as professional services or ongoing premium; dedicated CS - should be in premium tier or priced per engagement level; custom reporting - premium tier feature or separately priced module.
