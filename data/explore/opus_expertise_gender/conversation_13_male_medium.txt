### Assistant: Scaling from one to multiple rental properties involves transitions in almost every aspect of the business: how you find deals, how you finance them, how you manage tenants, and how you track finances. What works with one property often breaks at 5-10 properties. The biggest transitions: time management (you can't personally handle every maintenance call), systems (spreadsheets don't scale), deal flow (you need consistent ways to find properties), and capital (your approach to financing needs to evolve). How are you currently managing - self-managed, property manager? And what's your financing situation on the current property?

### Abstract: Your 5-10 hours monthly will likely become 20-30+ hours with 4-5 properties if you stay self-managed - that's when many investors hit a decision point about their time. The conventional mortgage path works but has limits: most lenders cap you at 4-10 financed properties personally, and each requires that 25% down, which limits how fast you can scale. At some point, you'll either need more creative financing (DSCR loans, portfolio lenders, commercial financing, or partnerships) or substantial capital. For systems: transition to real estate-specific software like Stessa, Buildium, or AppFolio before you're drowning. Track each property's P&L separately. ### Abstract: $5,000-7,000 monthly across 10 properties means $500-700 per property per month net cash flow. That's achievable but be careful about linear extrapolation. Things that change at scale: vacancy compounds - one vacant property is an annoyance; two vacant properties simultaneously can stress your reserves. Budget for higher average vacancy as your portfolio grows. Management complexity - even if you self-manage, at 10 properties you're running a real business. One property has maybe 2-3 maintenance issues per month; 10 properties have 20-30. Your "5-10 hours a month" becomes untenable without systems or a property manager. Property management (typically 8-10% of rent) changes your cash flow math significantly. Capital requirements - 10 properties at 25% down could mean $250,000-500,000+ in down payments depending on your market, plus reserves. That's substantial capital to deploy. The path to 10 properties matters - buying one per year is very different from buying 5 in year one through creative financing. The first path is more conservative; the second requires more sophistication but scales faster. What's your timeline and current capital situation?
