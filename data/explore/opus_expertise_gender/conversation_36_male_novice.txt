### Human: I want to learn basic home maintenance but I grew up in apartments and never learned any of this stuff. What should I know?

### Abstract: Learning basic home maintenance is incredibly valuable and saves a lot of money on service calls for simple fixes. The good news is most common issues are pretty straightforward once you know what you're doing. Let's start with the essentials. Do you own or rent? If you rent, your landlord handles bigger stuff. Also, are we talking house or condo? Houses have more systems to worry about (roof, yard, exterior) while condos are mostly interior.

### Human: I just bought my first house. It's overwhelming - there's so much that could break and I have no idea how any of it works.

### Abstract: Congratulations on the house! It's normal to feel overwhelmed. Let's break it down into must-know basics: First, find and label your main shut-offs. Water main shut-off (usually in basement or near street) - this stops flooding from any plumbing disaster. Electrical panel (breaker box) - know how to flip breakers and which ones control what. Gas shut-off if you have gas appliances. Second, learn these common fixes: unclogging drains (plunger, then drain snake), replacing a toilet flapper (super easy, $5 part), resetting a tripped breaker, changing air filters (do this every 1-3 months!), and relighting a pilot light if you have gas appliances. YouTube tutorials walk you through everything step by step. Start there before calling professionals.

### Human: What about bigger problems? How do I know when I need a professional versus doing something myself?

### Abstract: Good rule of thumb: if water, gas, or electricity could seriously hurt you or damage your home, start with a professional until you're confident. Specifically: call a pro for anything involving your main electrical panel (not just switching breakers), gas line work, main water line issues, roofing problems, structural concerns, or HVAC repairs beyond filter changes. DIY is generally fine for: clogged drains, running toilets, replacing light fixtures and outlets (after turning off power), minor drywall patches, caulking around tubs and windows, painting, and most cosmetic fixes. When you're unsure, watch a YouTube video first. If it looks manageable, try it. If it seems sketchy, call someone. Also pro tip: build a relationship with a good handyman for the in-between jobs - they're cheaper than specialists for small stuff and can often tell you what you can do yourself versus what needs a pro. Ask neighbors for recommendations.
