### Human: I've been playing disc golf for about 2 years and I've developed a pretty consistent backhand but my forehand is really inconsistent. Some days it's great, other days I can't throw it at all. What am I probably doing wrong?

### Assistant: Forehand consistency issues usually come from one of a few root causes: grip inconsistency, release point variability, or relying on arm/wrist rather than core rotation for power. The "some days great, some days terrible" pattern often indicates that you don't have the mechanics fully locked in - when everything happens to align, it works; when one variable is off, it falls apart. Backhand is more forgiving of slight variations because the motion is more natural for most people. What does your forehand feel like on bad days - does it grip wrong, turn over, nose up, fade early?

### Human: On bad days it usually turns over and dumps left (I'm right-handed) or feels like it has no spin. I can't seem to figure out what I'm doing differently from good days.

### Abstract: Turning over and low spin on bad days points to a few likely culprits: wrist roll on release - the forehand relies on keeping your wrist on plane through the hit. If your wrist rolls over at release, the disc turns over immediately. On good days, you're probably holding the wrist flat through the hit naturally; on bad days, something in your timing or tension is causing it to roll. Power leaking forward instead of spinning - forehand power comes from the core rotating and the arm whipping through. If you're "pushing" the disc forward instead of spinning it, you get less spin and more turn. Grip pressure - forehand grip needs to be firmer than many players expect, especially the pressure between thumb and the middle finger (which should be on the rim). Loose grip leads to the disc wobbling out without spin. Fixes: focus on "pouring a pitcher" with your follow-through - your palm should face up after release, not roll over. This cue helps maintain wrist position. Slow down your run-up and focus on timing rather than power. Many forehand inconsistencies come from the lower and upper body getting out of sync. Record yourself on good and bad days - even your phone in slow-mo can reveal what's different.

### Human: I don't really have a run-up for forehand - I mostly just plant and throw from standstill. Should I be adding footwork?

### Abstract: Standstill forehand is fine for many situations and actually helps consistency since there are fewer moving parts. Adding footwork can increase power but often decreases consistency, especially if your standstill isn't locked in yet. I'd actually recommend staying with standstill until your mechanics are solid. However, your standstill setup might be part of the problem. Common standstill forehand issues: not engaging the hips - even from standstill, power should come from hip rotation driving the arm. If you're arm-only throwing, you're losing both power and consistency. Stance too narrow or too wide - your feet position affects how well you can rotate. Shoulder leading instead of hips - the sequence should be hips fire first, pulling the shoulder, which pulls the arm. If you start with the arm/shoulder, you lose the whip effect. For standstill forehand: set up with your lead foot (throwing side) slightly forward, weight on back foot. Shift weight forward as you rotate hips, let arm follow. The arm is the last thing to move, not the first. Practice with putters or slow midranges first - they're more forgiving and give you feedback on whether you're getting clean spin. Once the motion is consistent, move to faster discs.
