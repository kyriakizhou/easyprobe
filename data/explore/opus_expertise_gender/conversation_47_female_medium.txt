### Human: I've been managing our household renovations for about 5 years and realized I'm better at this than my contractor. I'm thinking about becoming a project manager for residential construction. Is that a thing, and how would I get into it?

### Assistant: It's absolutely a thing, and your owner-side experience is valuable! Residential project management roles exist in: custom home builders (managing builds for clients), renovation companies (overseeing multiple projects), design-build firms, and as independent project managers/owner's reps hired by homeowners. The skill set is real: scheduling, budgeting, contractor coordination, problem-solving, and client management. What specifically do you enjoy most about managing renovations?

### Human: I love the problem-solving and coordination aspects. Figuring out sequencing, managing multiple trades, keeping things on budget. The design side is less interesting to me.

### Assistant: Those are core PM competencies! The execution focus versus design is actually an advantage - many PMs get pulled into design opinions when their value is managing process. For getting into the field: your homeowner experience shows aptitude but you'll need to demonstrate construction knowledge to employers. Options: entry-level coordinator position at a builder (learning their systems while proving yourself), assistant PM role, or residential construction management courses to formalize knowledge. CMAA, AGC, and local community colleges offer relevant certifications and courses. Do you have any relationships with contractors or builders from your renovation work?

### Human: I know some contractors pretty well but they're small operators, not companies with PM positions. How do I find the actual opportunities?

### Assistant: Look for: custom home builders (higher-end builders often have PM roles), renovation companies with multiple concurrent projects, design-build firms, and general contractors specializing in residential. Search for: construction project manager, project coordinator, construction scheduler, owner's representative. Also look at real estate development companies. Network beyond your small contractors: attend homebuilder association meetings, connect on LinkedIn with people in these roles, and ask your contractors who the good builders in your area are. Some people start by offering owner's rep services directly to homeowners - being the organized professional who manages their renovation for a fee.

### Human: What about the technical knowledge? I've learned a lot but I don't have formal construction training.

### Assistant: Your practical experience matters, but filling gaps helps. Areas to strengthen: building codes and permitting processes, construction sequencing and dependencies, reading plans and specs, estimating basics, and construction contracts and insurance. Ways to learn: community college construction management courses, online certifications, NAHB (National Association of Home Builders) courses, shadowing your contractor contacts, and books like "Managing the Construction Process." You don't need an engineering degree for residential PM work, but demonstrating foundational knowledge matters. During interviews, your homeowner experience shows you understand client perspective - that's valuable combined with technical learning.

### Human: Realistically, what would starting pay look like?

### Assistant: Residential construction PM salaries vary by region and company size. Entry-level/coordinator positions: $45-60K. Experienced residential PMs: $65-90K. High-end custom home PMs in expensive markets: $90-120K+. Benefits vary - some are employee positions, others are hourly/contract. Independent owner's representatives charge fees or percentages (typically 3-10% of project cost). The field generally pays less than commercial construction PM but offers: projects that feel personal and tangible, variety and problem-solving, and potentially better work-life balance than commercial. Your specific market matters a lot - research local job postings to understand regional rates.
