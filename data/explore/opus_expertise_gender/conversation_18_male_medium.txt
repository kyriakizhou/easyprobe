### Human: I've been homebrewing mead for about 2 years and getting decent results with traditional meads. I want to try melomels (fruit meads) but I'm not sure about the best approach to adding fruit without getting too much astringency or off-flavors.

### Assistant: Melomels add complexity but the principles aren't dramatically different from traditional mead. The timing, amount, and type of fruit matter significantly for the final product. The main concerns: astringency from skins and seeds, loss of fresh fruit character from primary fermentation, and potential for stuck fermentation from wild yeast/bacteria on fruit. What fruits are you interested in using, and are you doing step-feeding or TOSNA nutrient protocols?

### Human: I've been doing TOSNA for the last year with good results. Thinking of starting with berry melomels - probably blackberry or raspberry. I've heard about adding fruit in primary versus secondary and I'm not sure which approach is better.

### Abstract: With TOSNA experience, you're well-prepared for melomels. Berry fruits work great for beginners because they're forgiving and the flavors come through clearly. Primary versus secondary fruit addition: in primary - you get more fermentation of fruit sugars, which can add complexity and depth. The fruit character is more "winey" and integrated. But you lose fresh fruit brightness and aromatics, which blow off during fermentation. In secondary - fruit flavor is brighter and fresher, more recognizable as the fruit. Less integrated but often more appealing in berry melomels where you want that fresh fruit pop. Both approaches work, and many meadmakers do both: some fruit in primary for body and depth, fresh fruit in secondary for aroma and brightness. For berries specifically, I'd lean toward secondary addition or a split approach. The fresh berry character is usually what you want to showcase. Amounts: 2-4 lbs per gallon is typical for berries. More gives more fruit character but can overwhelm honey. Start around 3 lbs/gallon for secondary addition.

### Human: How do I avoid contamination from wild yeast on the fruit? I've heard freezing helps but does it actually work?

### Abstract: Contamination risk from fruit is real but manageable. Freezing does help - it kills some (not all) wild yeast and bacteria, and it breaks down cell walls which helps juice extraction. It's not sterilization but it's useful. Other approaches: campden tablets (potassium metabisulfite) - one crushed tablet per gallon 24 hours before adding fruit kills most wild organisms. This is widely used and effective. Some people do this in addition to freezing. Pasteurization - heating fruit to 150-160°F briefly kills wild yeast. Works but can change fruit character and set pectin (haze). Generally not preferred for melomels. Sanitation, not sterilization - your healthy fermentation with active yeast creates an environment that outcompetes most contamination. A strong established yeast population is your best defense. For secondary addition specifically: make sure your primary fermentation is complete (or nearly so) and your yeast population is established before adding fruit. The existing yeast will dominate. If adding to secondary after racking off primary, consider adding fresh yeast nutrients (not a full TOSNA schedule) to support the yeast that transferred. My approach for berries: freeze fruit, thaw, add to secondary (rack onto it), possibly add campden 24 hours before fruit if you're paranoid. This balances safety and fruit character.
