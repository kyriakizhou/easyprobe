### Human: I want to start reading more but I keep getting distracted and never finish books. Any tips?

### Abstract: Struggling to finish books is really common, especially in the age of phones and constant notifications. The good news is reading is like a muscle - it gets easier the more you do it. A few questions first: what kind of books are you trying to read? How are you typically trying to read - physical book, e-reader, phone app? And when/where do you attempt to read? These details help me suggest what might work for you.

### Human: Mostly self-help and business books. Usually on my phone, usually in bed before sleep. I get like 10 pages in then end up on TikTok somehow.

### Abstract: Ah, that's the problem right there - your phone is an attention-stealing machine, and you're trying to read on the same device that has TikTok. You're fighting a losing battle. Fix number one: switch to physical books or a dedicated e-reader (Kindle) that doesn't have social media. The device matters more than willpower. Fix number two: keep your phone in another room while reading, or at least across the room. Out of reach, out of mind. Also, some self-help and business books are honestly pretty dry and repetitive - it's okay to acknowledge that not every book is compelling. You might enjoy reading more with different genres. Have you tried fiction? Many avid readers got hooked through stories, not self-improvement. Pick something that sounds genuinely fun, not something you feel like you "should" read.

### Human: How do I actually build the habit of reading regularly? I've tried before and it never sticks.

### Abstract: Building a reading habit comes down to making it easy and consistent. Start embarrassingly small - commit to just 10 pages or 10 minutes a day, not an hour. You can always read more, but the goal is consistency. Attach it to an existing habit: read with your morning coffee, read on your lunch break, read before bed (but with phone out of the room). Same time every day helps it stick. Keep a book visible and accessible - leave it on your nightstand, couch, or wherever you'll see it. Track your progress - apps like Goodreads let you set goals and mark books as read, which feels satisfying. Start with page-turners that are hard to put down rather than challenging books. Give yourself permission to quit books you don't enjoy - forcing yourself through boring books kills the habit. If you read even 15 minutes daily, that's over 20 books a year for most people.
