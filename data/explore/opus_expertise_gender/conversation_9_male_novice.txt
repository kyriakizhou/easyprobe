### Human: I want to start playing guitar but I don't know if I should buy an acoustic or electric. Which is better for beginners?

### Assistant: Both are perfectly fine for beginners - it really depends on what music you want to play! If you want to play rock, metal, blues, or anything with distortion, go electric. If you're more into folk, country, singer-songwriter stuff, or just want to play around campfires, acoustic makes sense. There's an old myth that you should start acoustic then switch to electric, but that's not true. Starting on whichever one excites you most will keep you motivated to practice. ### Human: I really want to learn rock and classic rock songs. So I guess electric makes more sense. But don't you need a bunch of other equipment with an electric guitar?

### Assistant: For rock and classic rock, electric is definitely the way to go! Yes, you need a few extra things, but it's not as complicated or expensive as people think. You'll need: the guitar itself, an amplifier (small practice amps are fine and cheap), a cable to connect them, and some picks. A beginner bundle deal from brands like Squier (Fender's budget line), Epiphone (Gibson's budget line), or Yamaha often includes guitar, amp, cable, strap, and picks for $200-350 total. That's everything you need to start. You don't need pedals or fancy gear right away - just the basics. The amp will have some built-in distortion to get that rock sound.

### Human: How long before I can actually play songs? I'm worried I'll lose interest if it takes forever to do anything fun.

### Abstract: Great news - you can play recognizable songs within weeks, not months or years! Many rock songs are built on just a few chords. Songs like "Smoke on the Water," "Seven Nation Army," and "Iron Man" use simple riffs that beginners can learn in days. After a month of regular practice (20-30 minutes a day), you could probably play several easy songs. Yes, it takes years to master guitar, but the early wins come fast enough to stay motivated. The key is picking songs you actually like rather than boring exercises. Apps like Yousician or Justin Guitar's free lessons can guide you through beginner songs step by step. What matters most is consistent practice - even 15 minutes daily beats an hour once a week.
