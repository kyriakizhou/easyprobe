### Human: I need to give a presentation at work next week and I'm terrified of public speaking. Any advice?

### Assistant: Public speaking anxiety is incredibly common - you're definitely not alone. The good news is that preparation can make a huge difference in how confident you feel. First, how long is the presentation and what's it about? And is this to a small team or a larger audience? Knowing more helps me give you specific advice.

### Human: It's about 15 minutes, presenting a project update to my department - maybe 20 people. I've never presented to this many people before.

### Assistant: A 15-minute project update is very manageable! Here's how to prepare: know your material cold - practice out loud at least 5-6 times, not just reading silently. Time yourself to make sure you hit 15 minutes. Create simple slides with minimal text - they should support you, not be a script you read from. Structure it clearly: quick overview, main points, next steps, questions. For nerves: remember that these are your colleagues who want you to succeed. They're there for the information, not to judge you. Take slow deep breaths before you start. Have water nearby. It's okay to pause. Most people can't tell you're nervous even when you feel like it's obvious.

### Human: What if I forget what I'm supposed to say? That's my biggest fear.

### Assistant: Forgetting your words is a super common fear, but there are strategies to prevent it. First, don't memorize a script word-for-word - that's when you freeze if you lose your place. Instead, memorize the key points and transitions. Use your slides as visual cues that remind you what comes next. Keep brief notes with bullet points you can glance at - nobody will judge you for checking notes. If you do blank out: pause, take a breath, look at your notes or slide. You can say "Let me check my notes on this" - totally professional. Or just move to the next point you do remember. The audience doesn't know what you planned to say, so they won't notice if you skip something. Practice with a friend who can help you recover from intentional interruptions.
