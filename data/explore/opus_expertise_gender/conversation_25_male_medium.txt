### Human: I've been doing SEO for my company's website for about 2 years and I've got the basics down - keyword research, on-page optimization, building some backlinks. Traffic has plateaued though and I'm not sure what I'm missing.

### Assistant: Traffic plateaus after initial SEO gains are common. The fundamentals get you to a certain level, and then you need more sophisticated approaches. The usual plateau causes: content quality ceiling (you've optimized existing content but haven't added substantial new content), competitive keyword saturation (you're ranking for terms you can, but better terms have stronger competition), technical limitations you haven't addressed, or your backlink profile isn't strong enough for higher-competition terms. What does your traffic trend look like - completely flat, growing slowly, or actually declining? And what's your content publishing cadence?

### Human: Traffic grew steadily for the first year then has been basically flat for 8 months. We publish maybe 2-3 blog posts per month but they're not getting much organic traffic. Most of our traffic goes to our main service pages.

### Abstract: That pattern suggests your service pages are well-optimized but your content strategy isn't working effectively. 2-3 blog posts monthly is decent volume, but if they're not attracting traffic, there's likely a disconnect between what you're publishing and what people search for. Common issues: keyword targeting - are these blog posts targeted at actual search queries with volume, or topics your team thinks are interesting? Big difference. Search intent mismatch - informational posts targeting commercial queries or vice versa. Content depth - thin posts that don't compete with what's ranking. Promotion - new content needs backlinks and distribution to rank; publishing alone isn't enough. Audit your recent posts: pull them into Search Console and check impressions and positions. Posts with impressions but low positions have potential - they're being considered by Google but not ranking well. Posts with zero impressions might be targeting keywords you have no chance at. For the service pages that perform well: what's linking to them, what's their domain authority, and have you built out supporting content (hub and spoke) around those topics?

### Human: I haven't really thought about content strategy systematically - we've mostly written about topics our team thinks are useful without much keyword research for the blog. How should I approach content planning differently?

### Abstract: That's a common pattern and likely your biggest opportunity. Shifting to search-driven content planning: start with topic research, not brainstorming. Use tools (Ahrefs, SEMrush, even free tools like Ubersuggest) to find what people actually search for in your space. Look for keywords with decent volume that you could realistically rank for. Map content to funnel stage. Informational content (how-to guides, explanations) builds traffic and awareness but converts indirectly. Commercial content (comparisons, "best X for Y") drives more direct conversions. You need both, intentionally. Build topic clusters around your main service pages. If you rank well for "commercial insurance" (example), create supporting content for "types of commercial insurance," "commercial insurance cost factors," "commercial insurance for [industry]." Internal link these to your service page. This signals topical authority. Prioritize ruthlessly. You can't write everything. Focus on keywords where: there's actual search volume, the competition is beatable (check what's ranking - can you do better?), and the topic aligns with your business. Create a 90-day content calendar based on this research. Track which posts gain traction and double down on what works (update, expand, build related content). Kill the "our team thinks this is useful" approach unless it's also validated by search data.
