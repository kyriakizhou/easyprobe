### Human: I'm structuring a programmatic joint venture for acquiring and repositioning distressed retail real estate. The GP/LP structure needs to accommodate institutional investors with different return requirements and risk tolerances, plus an operating partner with sweat equity. How do you typically see these waterfalls structured?

### Assistant: Programmatic retail distress JVs require waterfall structures that align incentives across multiple parties with different objectives. Typical structure: institutional LPs seeking current yield plus upside, GP/sponsor seeking promote economics, and operating partner seeking carried interest on value creation. Common waterfall: preferred return to LPs (often 8-10% for distress strategies), return of capital, then promote splits that escalate with returns (70/30 LP/GP at first hurdle, 60/40 at second, 50/50 above higher threshold). Operating partner participation usually comes from GP's share through a co-promote arrangement. Key structural questions: is the preferred return compounding or simple? Is it cumulative or non-cumulative? Are there catch-up provisions for GP? Is promote calculated deal-by-deal or on a pooled basis across the program?

### Human: We're debating deal-by-deal versus pooled promote calculation. Institutional investors are pushing for pooled to avoid paying promote on winners while losers haven't been resolved. The operating partner wants deal-by-deal for earlier liquidity.

### Assistant: The deal-by-deal versus pooled tension is classic in programmatic JVs. Deal-by-deal: operating partner and GP get promote as individual deals monetize, but creates risk of promoting on early winners while later deals in the portfolio underperform (the "cherry-picking" problem for investors). Pooled/aggregated: all deals contribute to a single waterfall, promote only paid after total portfolio achieves hurdle returns, but creates timing mismatch where promotes may not be realized until end of program. Hybrid approaches: deal-by-deal promotes with clawback provisions that require GP/operating partner to return promotes if pooled returns ultimately fall short; interim promotes with holdback/escrow until portfolio-level returns are confirmed; or deal-by-deal below certain threshold, pooled above (aligns interests on large gains while providing some early liquidity). For distressed retail specifically, where individual asset outcomes can vary dramatically, institutional preference for pooled makes sense. What's the operating partner's capital contribution, if any?

### Human: Operating partner is contributing mostly sweat equity - sourcing, due diligence, active repositioning work - with minimal capital. They're arguing they shouldn't bear pooled risk when they don't have capital at risk.

### Assistant: The operating partner's argument has merit but cuts both ways. Sweat equity contributors bear opportunity cost and execution risk, which is real. However, from LP perspective: promote to a party without capital at risk creates misaligned incentives - the operating partner benefits from deal wins but doesn't share in capital losses on deal failures. Structural solutions: require operating partner to co-invest at least nominal capital alongside sweat equity (aligns interests and demonstrates conviction); tiered promote where operating partner's share is deal-by-deal up to a cap, with amounts above the cap pooled; or bifurcate operating partner compensation into management fees for ongoing work (not at risk) and promote only for above-target returns (at risk through pooling). Another approach: milestone-based payments to operating partner for specific value-creation achievements (lease-up targets, sale prices) that aren't tied to overall waterfall - this provides liquidity for their work without creating promote misalignment. What's the expected hold period and exit strategy for individual assets?

### Human: Three to five year holds typically, with disposition as assets are repositioned and stabilized. We expect some assets will recycle faster if we can't achieve repositioning targets.

### Assistant: Three-to-five year repositioning holds with variable exit timing creates complexity for pooled calculations. Considerations: if early exits are successes that generate promote and later exits are failures, deal-by-deal benefits GP/operator; if early exits are quick failures and later exits are successful repositioning, pooled benefits GP/operator. Neither party can predict the sequence, which argues for structural solutions rather than picking one approach. Recommended structure for your situation: deal-by-deal promotes with meaningful clawback and escrow. Specifically: promotes calculated on each disposition, but 25-30% of promote held in escrow until program wind-down or substantial completion; clawback obligation covering any escrow shortfall if pooled returns fall below hurdles; and operating partner guarantee of clawback obligation (to extent of prior distributions). This gives operating partner partial liquidity while protecting LPs against promoting on winners that subsidize losers. For the recycling scenario, be clear whether recycled capital resets the preferred return clock or continues accumulating - this affects economics significantly.

### Human: What about situation where we want to bring in a co-investor on specific larger deals within the program? How does that affect waterfall mechanics?

### Assistant: Co-investor accommodation is common in programmatic JVs but creates structural complexity. Approaches: pro-rata participation rights where existing LPs can co-invest alongside the program on larger deals (preserves LP relationships but may slow execution); designated co-investor allocations where specific LPs have rights to certain deal sizes or types; or third-party co-investor arrangements where the program syndicates excess size to outside capital. Waterfall implications: co-investor capital typically sits alongside or senior to program capital at the deal level, but promote calculations can vary. Options: co-investor capital included in program waterfall (dilutes GP/operator promote basis but simplifies administration), co-investor capital excluded from program waterfall with separate promote arrangement at deal level (preserves program economics but requires deal-by-deal negotiation), or co-investor pays fee/promote to program sponsor for access and execution (additional GP revenue stream). The key is defining co-investor rights and economics in your program documents upfront, even if specific deals aren't known - otherwise you'll negotiate these issues under time pressure when deals are live.
