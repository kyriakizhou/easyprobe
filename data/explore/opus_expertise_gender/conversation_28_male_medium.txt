### Human: I've been doing strength training for about 4 years and I'm interested in trying powerlifting competition. My current maxes are around 315/225/405 squat/bench/deadlift at 185lb bodyweight. Is that competitive enough to enter?

### Assistant: Your numbers at 185lb put you in a reasonable position to compete - 315/225/405 is solidly intermediate. More importantly: every level of powerlifting has competition. Local meets have everyone from first-timers with modest numbers to experienced lifters chasing records. The purpose of your first meet isn't to win - it's to experience the platform, learn the rules, and get an official total. Some people wait until their numbers are "good enough" and never compete. Others compete early and use it to fuel their training. The latter approach usually works better for long-term development. Have you trained the competition lifts with competition standards (pause on bench, depth on squat, commands)?

### Human: I train the movements but I don't always pause on bench or go competition depth on squat. Are the standards really different from what most people do in the gym?

### Abstract: The standards are different enough that you'll want to practice specifically. Competition requirements: squat - hip crease must pass below the top of the knee. Many gym squats that feel "deep enough" get red-lighted for depth. Video yourself from the side and be honest. You need to hit this consistently, not occasionally. Bench - commands are "start," then "press" after the bar touches and is motionless on your chest (the pause), then "rack." The pause is where most people fail - gym bench presses typically have a touch-and-go, but you need to show clear pause and wait for the press command. It changes the lift. Deadlift - you need to lock out completely (knees, hips, shoulders) and hold until the down command. No hitching (using your thighs to ramp the bar up). This is usually the easiest transition. Training adjustments: start pausing every bench rep now. Make competition depth your normal depth on squats, not your "going heavy" depth. Film yourself to verify. You'll probably need to back off your maxes initially as you adapt to stricter standards. How much time until you'd want to compete?

### Human: Probably 4-6 months. That seems like enough time to adjust. What should I know about choosing a meet and federation?

### Abstract: 4-6 months is good time to prepare if you start practicing standards now. Federation choice depends on your goals: USAPL is the largest tested federation in the US with the most consistent judging and clear pathway to national/international competition. Drug tested, stricter equipment rules (singlet, specific brands approved). USPA is large, offers both tested and untested divisions, more equipment options, somewhat more relaxed atmosphere at local meets. Various smaller federations (APF, RPS, WRPF affiliates) may have meets closer to you with different rules and testing policies. For your first meet, proximity and timing matter more than federation prestige. A local USAPL or USPA meet within driving distance is ideal. Look for "novice" or "first-timer" friendly meets if available. What to look for: check the federation's rules thoroughly - weigh-in procedures, commands, equipment requirements. Register early (meets fill up). Know your weight class and decide if you'll cut or compete at walking weight (don't cut for your first meet - competing without that stress is valuable). Most federations have online rules documents. Read them multiple times. Understanding commands and standards before meet day reduces stress enormously.
