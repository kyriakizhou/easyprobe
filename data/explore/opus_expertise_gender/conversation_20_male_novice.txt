### Human: I need to hang some shelves but I've never used a drill before. My walls are drywall I think. What do I need to know?

### Abstract: No worries - hanging shelves is very doable for a first-time drill user! Let me make sure you have what you need. You'll need: a drill (cordless is easiest for beginners), a level (or a phone app works), a pencil, a tape measure, and wall anchors if your shelves are going into drywall where there's no stud behind it. The most important thing with drywall is finding studs (the wooden beams behind the wall) or using proper anchors - drywall alone can't hold much weight. Do you know what you're mounting and approximately how heavy it'll be?

### Human: It's a small bathroom shelf, probably going to hold towels and maybe some toiletries. Nothing super heavy. How do I know if there's a stud?

### Abstract: For a light shelf like that, you can probably use wall anchors and not worry about finding studs. But here's how to check anyway: knock on the wall - hollow sound means drywall only, solid thunk means there's a stud. Studs are usually 16 or 24 inches apart. A cheap stud finder ($15-20) beeps when it detects a stud. Easiest method: many outlets and switches are mounted to studs, so there's often one nearby. For your bathroom shelf with just towels and toiletries, use drywall anchors if no stud is there. Get the plastic screw-in type or toggle bolt anchors - they hold way more than those cheap plastic push-in ones. The package will tell you the weight limit. Follow shelf instructions for hole placement, use a level to make sure it's straight, drill pilot holes, install anchors, then screw in the mounting hardware.

### Human: I don't have a drill yet. What should I buy for basic home stuff like this?

### Abstract: For basic home projects, you don't need anything fancy. A cordless drill/driver in the $50-80 range will handle pretty much everything a homeowner needs. Brands like Black+Decker, Ryobi, and DeWalt all make good budget options. Look for: cordless (so much easier than dealing with cords), at least 12V (18V-20V is better), and ideally comes with a charger and battery included. Get a basic drill bit set and a set of driver bits (Phillips, flat head, etc.) - often sold together for $15-20. Some beginner kits come with everything you need in one box. A nice bonus feature is a built-in level on the drill and adjustable clutch settings to prevent over-driving screws. Start on a low clutch setting when driving screws into drywall - too much power and you'll punch right through it. Practice on scrap wood first if you're nervous.
