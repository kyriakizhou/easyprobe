### Human: I want to get into whiskey but I have no idea where to start. It all tastes like burning to me right now.

### Abstract: The "burning" feeling is totally normal when you're starting out - your palate hasn't adjusted yet. Whiskey appreciation is genuinely a learned skill, not something you're born with. The good news is you can train your palate relatively quickly with the right approach. A few questions: have you tried different types of whiskey (bourbon, scotch, Irish, etc.)? And when you tried it, was it neat (straight), with ice, or in cocktails? How you drink it makes a big difference when starting out.

### Human: I think I've had Jack Daniels and some scotch someone gave me. Both were neat and both were rough. Everyone else seemed to enjoy them and I felt like I was missing something.

### Abstract: You're not missing anything - most people's first experiences with neat whiskey are exactly like yours! Starting neat with unfamiliar whiskey is like jumping into the deep end. Here's a better path: start with whiskey in cocktails or mixed drinks - an Old Fashioned or whiskey sour lets you taste the whiskey character without the full burn. Then try whiskey with ice or a splash of water - this opens up flavors and reduces the alcohol intensity significantly. As you get used to it, use less ice or water. For beginning, try Irish whiskey (Jameson, Tullamore DEW) - it's generally smoother and more approachable than bourbon or scotch. Bourbon tends to be sweeter, scotch can be smoky or complex. Jack Daniels is fine but not the smoothest introduction. Take small sips and let it sit on your tongue. Don't chase it or shoot it. The burning sensation decreases dramatically as you drink whiskey more often - your palate literally adapts.

### Human: So there's actually different types of whiskey? What's the difference?

### Abstract: Yes! Main types and their general characteristics: Bourbon - American, made primarily from corn, tends to be sweeter with notes of vanilla and caramel. Examples: Buffalo Trace, Maker's Mark, Evan Williams. Scotch - from Scotland, often has smoky or peaty flavors, especially from certain regions. Can range from smooth and honeyed to heavily smoky. Very diverse. Examples: Glenfiddich (smooth), Glenlivet (gentle), Laphroaig (super smoky - avoid for now). Irish whiskey - from Ireland, usually triple-distilled which makes it smoother and lighter. Often the best starting point. Examples: Jameson, Redbreast, Bushmills. Tennessee whiskey - like bourbon but charcoal-filtered (Jack Daniels is this). Rye - American, made with rye grain, tends to be spicier. Japanese whiskey - often similar to scotch, generally very smooth and refined. For starting out, I'd recommend Irish whiskey or a wheated bourbon (like Maker's Mark, which is softer). As you explore, you'll find what you like. There's no "correct" preference.
