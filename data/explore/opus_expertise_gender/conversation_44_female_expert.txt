### Human: I'm leading the implementation of model risk management for a regional bank that's grown rapidly and is now facing enhanced regulatory scrutiny. We have dozens of models across credit, market risk, BSA/AML, and operational risk, but model governance has been ad hoc. Where do I start?

### Assistant: Model risk management implementation at a bank that's grown past ad hoc governance requires balancing immediate regulatory needs against sustainable long-term framework. The regulatory expectation (SR 11-7 if you're US-based) covers model development, implementation, and use, with emphasis on validation, documentation, and governance. Start with: model inventory - you can't manage what you haven't identified. Catalog all models across credit, market, BSA/AML, operational risk. For each: what is it, who owns it, what decisions does it inform, when was it last validated, what documentation exists? This reveals scope of the problem and identifies highest-risk gaps. Risk tiering - not all models warrant equal scrutiny. Tier based on materiality (impact on decisions and financials), complexity, regulatory significance. High-tier models need immediate attention; lower-tier can follow. Current state gap assessment - against regulatory expectations and industry practice, where are your biggest gaps? Is it documentation, validation, change management, ongoing monitoring? What's triggered the enhanced regulatory scrutiny - was there a specific finding, or is it growth-related?

### Human: Growth-related - we crossed asset thresholds that put us in a higher supervisory category. Examiners have signaled they'll be looking at model risk management in the next exam. We have about 60 models and virtually no formal documentation or validation for most of them.

### Assistant: Sixty models with virtually no formal documentation or validation is a significant gap against regulatory expectations - you won't close it completely before examination, so prioritization is critical. Triage approach: immediate priority (next 3 months) - identify your 8-10 highest materiality models (likely: CECL/allowance model, credit scoring models, interest rate risk model, BSA/AML transaction monitoring). For these: create minimum viable documentation (model purpose, methodology, key assumptions, limitations, inputs/outputs), perform focused validation activities (performance testing, benchmark comparison, sensitivity analysis), and establish ongoing monitoring protocols. Secondary priority (3-6 months) - next tier of models, similar treatment but can proceed at slower pace. Governance infrastructure in parallel - establish model risk policy, define roles and responsibilities, create model inventory system, implement change management process. This shows examiners you have a framework even if every model isn't fully compliant yet. Examination strategy - acknowledge the gaps proactively, present your remediation plan with specific timelines and milestones, demonstrate progress on high-priority models. Examiners respond better to "we know the gaps and here's our plan" than to discovered deficiencies. Do you have internal model risk expertise, or will you need to build or borrow it?

### Human: Limited internal expertise - we have analysts who built and use the models, but no dedicated model validation function. We're considering whether to build internally, use consultants, or some hybrid.

### Assistant: Limited internal expertise with 60 models and exam pressure requires a hybrid approach - you can't build internal capability fast enough for immediate needs, but pure consultant reliance isn't sustainable or cost-effective. Recommended structure: near-term (consultant-heavy): engage model risk consultants to perform initial validation of high-priority models and help establish governance framework. This addresses immediate exam readiness while you build. Consultants with regulatory examination experience can also help you prepare for and navigate the exam itself. Medium-term (hybrid): hire 1-2 experienced model validation professionals to build internal function. They can work alongside consultants, learn your models, and gradually take ownership. Look for candidates with bank model validation experience who understand regulatory expectations. Long-term (internal with selective outsourcing): internal team handles ongoing validation, monitoring, and governance for most models. Consultants used for specialized expertise (complex quantitative models), capacity overflow, and independent review where fresh perspective is valuable. Hiring considerations: model validation professionals are in demand given regulatory focus across the industry. Competitive compensation and clear career path matter. Consider whether your location is a constraint - remote work may expand your talent pool. Cost framing: model risk management is a regulatory requirement, not optional. The cost of building the function is the cost of being in the banking business at your size. What's your timeline to examination, and has your regulator provided any specific guidance on expectations?

### Human: Examination is expected in about 6 months. No specific guidance yet, but we're expecting standard SR 11-7 expectations given our new supervisory category.

### Assistant: Six months to examination with SR 11-7 expectations is tight but manageable with focused execution.
