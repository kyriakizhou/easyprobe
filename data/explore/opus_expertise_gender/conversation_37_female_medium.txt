### Human: I've been doing wedding photography for about 4 years and I'm established but not premium. I want to raise my prices significantly but I'm worried about losing clients. How do I position myself higher?

### Assistant: Moving upmarket is as much about perception and positioning as it is about photography skill. Premium clients aren't just buying photos - they're buying an experience, confidence, and a certain aesthetic brand. What's your current price range, and how do you typically find clients?

### Human: I'm around $3,500 for full-day coverage. Most clients come from referrals and Instagram. I want to get to $5-6K range but feel like I'll price myself out of my current referral network.

### Assistant: That's a significant but achievable jump! You're right that your current referral network may not support premium pricing - they refer people like themselves. To move up, you need to reach a different market segment. Strategies: showcase work that attracts premium clients (editorial style, high-end venues, elevated aesthetics), feature on wedding blogs/publications that premium couples read (Grace Ormonde, Style Me Pretty), partner with luxury vendors who can refer up (planners, florists, venues), and curate your Instagram to reflect the market you want, not the market you have.

### Human: My portfolio is mixed - some beautiful venues, some simpler weddings. Should I be more selective about what I show?

### Assistant: Absolutely. Curate ruthlessly. Your portfolio should show only work that represents what you want to attract. If premium couples are your target, show: stunning venues, editorial-quality images, elevated details, beautiful couples and styling. Remove anything that looks budget or dated. This feels uncomfortable because you're proud of all your work, but you're not showing "best" photos - you're showing photos that attract your ideal client. If you don't have enough premium work yet, styled shoots at high-end venues can fill gaps. Your portfolio is marketing, not a comprehensive archive.

### Human: What about the client experience? I've heard that matters for premium.

### Assistant: Client experience is often the differentiator. Premium clients expect: elevated communication (responsive, professional, personalized), beautiful touchpoints (proposals, contracts, galleries presented beautifully), white-glove service (anticipating needs, smooth processes), and deliverables that feel luxurious (albums, prints, packaging). Evaluate your current process: does every touchpoint feel premium? Invest in: CRM tools for polished proposals (HoneyBook, Dubsado), professional branding, high-quality album samples, and maybe upgrading your attire and equipment presentation. The experience justifies the price; couples paying $6K expect to feel valued and guided through the process.

### Human: How do I make the transition without losing all my current bookings?

### Assistant: Gradual transition is wise. Strategies: raise prices incrementally with each booking cycle rather than all at once, maintain your current pricing for the referral network while pursuing premium leads separately, announce a price increase with a deadline ("booking at current rates through [date]"), and accept that losing some price-sensitive clients is part of moving upmarket - you'll book fewer weddings at higher rates. During transition, be selective about which current-price weddings you accept (prioritize beautiful venues that will photograph well for portfolio). Within 18-24 months you can fully transition if you're consistently landing higher-end work.
