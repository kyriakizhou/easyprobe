### Human: My car is making a weird noise when I brake. Is this something I can figure out myself or do I need a mechanic?

### Assistant: Brake noises definitely shouldn't be ignored, but let me help you narrow down what it might be. Can you describe the noise more - is it a high-pitched squeal, a grinding sound, or more of a thumping/pulsing feeling? And does it happen every time you brake or only sometimes? Also, does it change if you're braking lightly versus hard?

### Human: It's kind of a grinding sound, especially when I brake harder. Happens pretty much every time now.

### Assistant: A grinding sound during braking is a warning sign that shouldn't be put off. It usually means your brake pads have worn down to the metal and are now grinding against the rotors. This is beyond DIY territory for most beginners - you need a mechanic to look at it soon, ideally within a day or two. Continuing to drive on grinding brakes can damage your rotors (expensive) and compromise your stopping ability (dangerous). The good news is brake pad replacement is a common repair and not usually too expensive. Get a few quotes from local shops. In the meantime, drive gently and leave extra stopping distance.

### Human: How much should I expect to pay? I don't want to get ripped off.

### Assistant: Brake pad replacement typically runs $150-300 per axle (front or rear) including parts and labor. If your rotors are damaged from the grinding, that adds $150-400 more per axle depending on your car. Some tips to avoid getting overcharged: get quotes from 2-3 shops before committing. Ask specifically what's included - some places quote just the pads, then add fees later. Check online reviews for shops with good reputations. Dealerships are usually most expensive; independent shops and chains like Brake Masters or Midas are often cheaper. When they inspect it, ask to see the old parts - a reputable shop will show you the worn pads. Don't let anyone pressure you into additional services you didn't come in for.
