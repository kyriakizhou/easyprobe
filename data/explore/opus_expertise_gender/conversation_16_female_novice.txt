### Human: Hi! My daughter is getting married and I've been asked to give a speech. I've never spoken in front of a crowd before and I'm terrified. How do I not mess this up?

### Assistant: Congratulations on your daughter's wedding! It's completely natural to be nervous about public speaking - most people are. The wonderful thing about a mother-of-the-bride speech is that everyone in the room already wants you to succeed and is there to celebrate love. What specifically worries you most - forgetting what to say, crying, or something else?

### Human: All of it! But mostly I'm afraid I'll freeze or start crying and not be able to finish. What if I blank out completely?

### Assistant: Both very common fears! Here are some tricks: Write out your speech and practice it many times - familiarity builds confidence. Bring note cards with key points (not the whole speech word-for-word) so if you blank, you can glance down. If you feel tears coming, pause, take a breath, and take a sip of water - it gives you a moment to compose yourself. And honestly? If you do cry a little, that's okay. Happy tears at a wedding are expected and touching.

### Human: How long should the speech be? I don't know what's normal.

### Assistant: For a mother-of-the-bride speech, 2-4 minutes is perfect - about 300-600 words when spoken. Long enough to be meaningful, short enough to hold attention. That's roughly 1-2 pages handwritten. Many people write too much and then feel pressured to rush through it. A short, heartfelt speech is always better than a long, rambling one. Quality over quantity!

### Human: What should I actually say? I don't know where to start.

### Assistant: Here's a simple structure that works beautifully: Start by welcoming everyone and thanking them for coming. Share a brief, sweet story or memory about your daughter (maybe something from childhood that relates to who she is now). Say something kind about your new son/daughter-in-law and why you're happy they found each other. End with a heartfelt wish for their future together and a toast. Keep it personal, positive, and from the heart. Avoid inside jokes only a few people will get.

### Human: This is really helpful. Any tips for the actual delivery?

### Assistant: Yes! Speak slower than feels natural - nerves make us speed up. Make eye contact with friendly faces in the crowd (your daughter, your partner, a supportive friend). Stand up straight and take a deep breath before you start. If your hands shake, rest them on the podium or hold your cards with both hands. Practice out loud at home multiple times - in front of a mirror or your partner. And remember: you're not performing for strangers. You're speaking from your heart to people who love you and your daughter. They're rooting for you!
