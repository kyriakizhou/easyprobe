### Human: I'm leading due diligence on a Series B healthcare SaaS company. Revenue is growing but customer acquisition cost metrics seem too good compared to benchmarks. What financial forensics should I be applying to validate their numbers?

### Assistant: Healthy skepticism on CAC metrics is warranted - it's one of the most commonly manipulated numbers in SaaS. Several areas to probe: how they're defining and allocating sales and marketing costs (are they excluding certain costs that arguably should be included?), whether they're capitalizing costs that should be expensed, and timing games between spend and recognized customers. Also important: how are they counting "customers"? Free trials, heavily discounted early adopters, and partner-acquired customers can inflate customer counts while obscuring true CAC. What's the company's sales motion and ACV range?

### Human: Mid-market focus, $50-80K ACV, primarily inside sales with some partner channel. They're claiming CAC around $25K which would be exceptional efficiency for their segment.

### Assistant: At that ACV with inside sales, $25K CAC is indeed aggressive - typical would be 0.5-1x ACV for mid-market. Specific diligence areas: get the detailed CAC calculation and challenge every exclusion. Common games: excluding founder sales (if founders still sell, that's a cost), excluding certain marketing spend categories, not allocating appropriate overhead, or timing spend to periods before customer recognition. Cohort the CAC by acquisition channel - is that $25K average hiding $15K partner customers and $40K direct? Partner channel often has lower CAC but different payback and retention characteristics. Also verify customer counts against contracts - are some "customers" actually pilots or proof-of-concepts?

### Human: What about the relationship between CAC and lifetime value? Their LTV/CAC ratio looks great but I'm wondering if LTV is overstated.

### Assistant: LTV is even more manipulable than CAC. Key questions: what's their churn assumption in LTV calculation? (early-stage companies often use optimistic churn assumptions without enough data), are they using gross margin or revenue in LTV? (revenue-based LTV inflates the number), and what's their expansion revenue assumption? (assuming high expansion without historical proof is common). Request cohort retention curves - actual retention by customer cohort over time, not just company-wide average churn rate. Particularly in healthcare, early adopters may be stickier than the broader market, so projecting from early cohort retention can be misleading. Also: are there any concentration risks that inflate apparent retention?

### Human: Good points. They have one customer that's 18% of ARR. How concerning is that in the context of their metrics?

### Assistant: 18% concentration at Series B is a yellow flag that directly impacts your metric analysis. That customer's retention behavior dominates your portfolio churn numbers. Questions: how is that customer counted in cohort analysis? What's the contract status - if they're at renewal risk or planning to renegotiate, your LTV assumptions could be materially wrong. For diligence: exclude that customer from metrics and see how the picture changes. If the economics look significantly worse without them, the true business health is different from presented. Also understand the customer relationship - is this a lighthouse account that could drive future deals, or an outlier that won't be replicated?

### Human: What's the conversation with management about this? I don't want to be adversarial but need to pressure-test.

### Assistant: Frame it as collaborative rigor rather than gotcha. Approach: "We're seeing strong metrics that we want to understand deeply so we can support your story to our partnership. Help us walk through the methodology." Experienced operators expect and respect financial scrutiny - defensiveness is itself a signal. Specific asks: detailed CAC waterfall with allocation methodology, cohort retention data (not just summary metrics), documentation of how metrics are calculated, and pipeline analysis for forward visibility. Compare their metrics to audited financials where possible - CAC components should reconcile to S&M expense, customer counts should match contract documents. Discrepancies aren't necessarily fraud but require explanation. The goal is understanding, not accusation.
