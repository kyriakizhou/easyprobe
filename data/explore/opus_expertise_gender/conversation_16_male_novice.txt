### Human: I want to start grilling but I've only ever cooked indoors. I don't even know the difference between gas and charcoal. What should I get?

### Abstract: Welcome to grilling! The gas versus charcoal debate is passionate among grill enthusiasts, but for a beginner, here's the honest breakdown: Gas grills are way more convenient - turn a knob and you're cooking in 10 minutes, easy temperature control, simple cleanup. Charcoal takes longer to start (20-30 minutes), requires more skill to manage temperature, but gives you that classic smoky flavor. For a beginner who wants to actually use their grill regularly, I usually recommend starting with gas. You can always add a charcoal grill later once you get the basics down. What's your budget looking like?

### Human: Maybe $300-400? Nothing crazy. And I have a small patio so it can't be huge.

### Abstract: For that budget and a small patio, you've got good options. Weber Spirit series is the gold standard for reliable entry-level gas grills, usually around $400-500 new but often on sale or available used. Char-Broil and Nexgrill make solid options in the $300-400 range at Home Depot. Look for at least two burners (three is better), sturdy construction, and decent grate size. Don't cheap out too much on the grill itself - a $150 grill often rusts out or has hot spots. For a small patio, a compact two-burner model is plenty. Avoid really big grills that eat up your space. Also budget maybe $50-100 for accessories: a good instant-read meat thermometer (essential!), long-handled tongs and spatula, and a grill brush for cleaning.

### Human: What's easy to cook when I'm just starting out? I've heard horror stories about dried out chicken.

### Abstract: Start with forgiving foods and work up from there. Easiest beginner wins: burgers (hard to mess up, cook to 160°F internal), hot dogs and sausages (just rotate occasionally), vegetables like corn on the cob, zucchini, and peppers (brush with oil, season, grill until charred in spots). For chicken, the key is indirect heat for bone-in pieces and not overcooking. Chicken breast is actually tricky because it dries out fast - try thighs instead, they're more forgiving and juicier. Use that meat thermometer: chicken is done at 165°F internal. Steaks are easier than people think - season with salt and pepper, high heat, don't flip constantly. Medium rare is 130-135°F internal. The biggest beginner mistakes are: too high heat for everything, not preheating the grill, and not letting meat rest after cooking. Start with simple stuff and build confidence.
