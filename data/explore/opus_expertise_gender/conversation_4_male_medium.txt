### Assistant: Three years in with stalled progress is a common plateau point. A few potential culprits: the same program that drove initial gains may not be optimal anymore - intermediate lifters typically need more volume, different progression schemes, or periodization that beginner programs don't include. Your "eating enough" and "sleeping okay" might need to be scrutinized more carefully. And accumulated fatigue or minor technique inefficiencies compound over time. ### Human: Bench is stuck around 235, squat around 315, deadlift around 385. Bodyweight is 185. I try to add 5lbs when I can hit my target reps, but lately I can't hit targets. I took a week off a couple months ago but that's it.

### Assistant: Those are solid intermediate numbers at 185 - not beginner territory anymore. Linear progression (adding weight when you hit reps) typically stops working around this point. Your central nervous system and muscles need different stimuli. Several things to consider: periodization - instead of trying to add weight every session, work in blocks. A strength phase (lower reps, higher intensity), a volume phase (more sets, moderate weight), then test maxes. Programs like 5/3/1, GZCL, or Juggernaut are built for this. Volume adjustments - intermediate lifters often need more total sets per muscle group per week than beginner programs provide. If you're doing 10 sets of chest per week, try 15-18. Deloading more strategically - one week every 4-6 weeks at 50-60% intensity helps manage accumulated fatigue. Just taking time off doesn't accomplish the same thing as active deloading. Technical refinement - at these weights, small inefficiencies matter. Have you had coaching or video review of your lifts recently?

### Human: I haven't had anyone look at my form in a while. I post form checks sometimes but the feedback varies. How do I know if technique is actually limiting me?

### Abstract: Technique assessment at intermediate levels is tricky because the issues are subtle - not obviously "wrong" but slightly suboptimal. Signs technique might be limiting you: your strength differs significantly between similar movements (like stronger conventional deadlift but weak sumo suggests technique-dependent), you feel certain lifts in different muscles than you should (bench mostly in shoulders instead of chest), or you consistently miss lifts at the same sticking point. The best way to know: get an in-person session with a qualified coach or experienced powerlifter. Even one or two sessions can identify things that video form checks miss - stuff like bar path, bracing, timing, and subtle positioning. If that's not accessible, post form checks in more specialized communities (r/powerlifting, r/weightroom) where the feedback tends to be higher quality, and video from multiple angles (side view is essential but 45-degree front angle also helps). Record sets at around 80-85% of max - light enough that you're not grinding but heavy enough that technique issues show up. What does your bench setup look like specifically - that's often where the most technique gains are available?
