### Human: I need to write a resume but I've only had one job since college and I don't know how to make it look good. Any tips?

### Assistant: Having just one job isn't a problem at all - quality matters more than quantity. Let's focus on making that experience shine. Tell me about your role - what's your job title, how long have you been there, and what do you actually do day to day? Also, what kind of job are you applying for? That'll help me suggest what to highlight.

### Human: I've been a customer service rep for about 3 years. I answer calls, help customers with problems, process returns. I want to move into something more like an office admin or coordinator role.

### Assistant: Three years of customer service experience actually gives you a lot of transferable skills for admin roles! The key is framing your experience in terms of results and relevant skills. Instead of just listing duties, think about: how many customers did you help daily? Did you ever train new employees? Have you used any software or databases? Did you receive good reviews or meet performance targets? Turn these into bullet points like "Resolved 50+ customer inquiries daily while maintaining 95% satisfaction rating" or "Trained 3 new team members on company systems." Highlight organizational skills, communication, problem-solving, and any software you know. Put a skills section near the top listing things like Microsoft Office, data entry, scheduling, and communication.

### Human: How long should my resume be? I've heard one page but I don't know if I have enough to fill a page.

### Abstract: For someone with your experience level, one page is perfect and expected. Don't stress about filling the whole page - a clean, well-organized resume with white space looks more professional than one crammed with filler. Here's a good structure: contact info at the top, a brief 2-3 sentence summary highlighting your customer service experience and interest in admin roles, your work experience with 4-6 strong bullet points, then skills and education. If you're still short, you can add relevant coursework, any volunteer work, or professional development like online courses. But honestly, six solid bullet points that show real accomplishments are better than twelve vague ones. Focus on quality over trying to fill space.
