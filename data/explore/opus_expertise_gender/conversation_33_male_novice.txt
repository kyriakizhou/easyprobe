### Human: I moved to a new city and don't know anyone. How do I make friends as an adult? This is way harder than it was in school.

### Abstract: You're definitely not alone in feeling this - making friends as an adult is genuinely harder than school or college where you had built-in social structures. The key is creating repeated exposure to the same people, which requires being intentional about it since it doesn't happen automatically anymore. A few questions: what are your interests and hobbies? Are you more introverted or extroverted? And what kind of work situation are you in - remote, office, hybrid? These help me suggest approaches that'll actually fit your personality.

### Human: I'm pretty introverted honestly. I work remotely so I don't have coworkers to hang out with. I like gaming, hiking, and watching sports.

### Abstract: Those are actually great interests for meeting people! For introverts, smaller group activities work better than big social events. Some ideas based on your interests: join a local hiking group on Meetup.com - hiking is perfect for introverts because you're side-by-side, not face-to-face, and there's natural conversation or comfortable silence while walking. Find a sports bar with regulars who watch games - go for the same games consistently and you'll start recognizing faces and chatting. For gaming, look for local board game groups (many game shops host game nights) or Discord servers for your area where online friends sometimes do meetups. The key for introverts: pick one or two things and show up consistently. Friendship takes repeated exposure over time, not one great conversation. It feels awkward at first and that's totally normal. You're essentially dating for friends.

### Human: How do you go from seeing someone at an activity to actually being friends though? That transition feels weird.

### Abstract: That transition is honestly the hardest part. Here's how it works: after seeing someone a few times at an activity and having good conversations, suggest something specific and low-pressure. Something like "Hey, I'm going to check out [specific bar] for the game Sunday - you should come if you're free" or "Want to grab a beer after the next hike?" Making it specific and casual is key - not "we should hang out sometime" which is vague. Be the one who initiates. Many people feel exactly like you and would say yes but won't be the one to ask. It might take a few attempts, and not everyone will become a friend, and that's okay. Also, don't overthink it - if you've chatted a few times and they seem cool, they probably think you're cool too. The awkwardness of asking is temporary. Once you've hung out outside the activity once or twice, you've crossed the threshold from "activity acquaintance" to "actual friend." Exchange numbers, keep making plans. It gets easier.
