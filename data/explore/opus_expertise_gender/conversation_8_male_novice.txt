### Human: I want to start running but every time I try, I can only go for like a minute before I'm completely out of breath. Am I just not built for running?

### Assistant: You're absolutely built for running - you're just starting too fast! This is the most common mistake new runners make. When you're starting out, your "running" pace should be barely faster than walking. Seriously, if you can't hold a conversation while running, you're going too fast. Your cardiovascular system needs time to adapt. Have you heard of the Couch to 5K program? It's designed exactly for people starting from zero.

### Human: I've heard of it but never looked into it. Does it actually work?

### Abstract: Couch to 5K (C25K) is genuinely excellent for beginners - millions of people have used it successfully. It's a 9-week program that starts with mostly walking and short jogging intervals, gradually building up. Week 1 might be something like "run 60 seconds, walk 90 seconds, repeat." By the end, you're running 30 minutes straight. The magic is that it builds your fitness so gradually that it never feels impossible. There are free C25K apps that tell you when to walk and run so you don't have to think about it. The key is actually following the program - don't skip ahead because you feel good. Even fit people who are new to running benefit from starting slow.

### Human: What kind of shoes do I need? I just have regular sneakers.

### Abstract: For starting out, your regular sneakers are probably fine as long as they're relatively new and comfortable. You don't need to invest in expensive running shoes until you know you're going to stick with it. That said, if your sneakers are old and worn down, or if you start noticing knee, shin, or foot pain after a few weeks, then it's time to upgrade. When you're ready, go to a running specialty store (not a general sports store) - they'll watch you walk or run and recommend shoes that match your foot type. Expect to spend $100-150 for good running shoes. Proper shoes can prevent injuries, so it's worth the investment once you're committed to running regularly.
