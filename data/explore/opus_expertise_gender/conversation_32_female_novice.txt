### Human: Hi there! I want to start decluttering my house but I'm overwhelmed. Every time I try, I just move stuff around and nothing changes. Where do I start?

### Assistant: Decluttering overwhelm is so common! The key is to start small and have a clear system. Most people fail because they try to do too much at once or don't have a plan for where things will go. First question: is this general clutter throughout the house, or are there specific problem areas driving you crazy?

### Human: It's everywhere! Closets, kitchen drawers, the garage is a disaster. I don't even know what we have anymore.

### Assistant: When it's everywhere, the temptation is to try to tackle everything at once - but that's what leads to moving piles around! Instead, pick one small space to start - just one drawer or one shelf. Complete that before moving on. This gives you a quick win and teaches you the system. I'd suggest starting with something visible that you use daily, like the kitchen junk drawer or a bathroom cabinet. Small success builds momentum.

### Human: Okay, one drawer. But how do I decide what to keep and what to throw away?

### Assistant: A helpful approach is to sort everything into three piles: Keep, Donate/Sell, and Trash. For each item ask: Do I use this? Do I love it? Would I buy it again today? If the answer is no to all three, it goes. Be honest - "but I might need it someday" is usually a trap. If you haven't used something in a year, you probably won't. For sentimental items, keep a small box of true treasures and let go of things you're keeping out of guilt.

### Human: What do I do with all the stuff I'm getting rid of? That's part of why I never finish.

### Assistant: Having a plan for items leaving is crucial! Have trash bags ready for garbage. For donations, keep a bag or box in your car and drop off weekly at Goodwill or similar. For selling, only if items are worth significant money - otherwise, the hassle outweighs the benefit. Schedule a charity pickup for furniture. The key is: items must actually LEAVE your house, not just go to another pile. Set a rule: donation items must be out within one week.

### Human: How do I keep it from getting cluttered again?

### Assistant: The "one in, one out" rule helps - when something new comes in, something old must go. Also, give everything a designated home so you always know where it belongs. After decluttering a space, be mindful about what you bring back in - do you really need 15 spatulas? Doing a 10-minute tidy each evening prevents buildup. And stop the influx: unsubscribe from catalogs, resist sale temptations, and pause before buying to ask "where will this live?" Prevention is easier than cure!
