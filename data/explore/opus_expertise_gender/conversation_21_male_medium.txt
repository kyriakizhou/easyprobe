### Human: I've been brewing espresso at home for about 2 years with a decent machine and grinder. I can pull decent shots but my consistency is all over the place. Some days it's great, other days it tastes off. What am I missing?

### Assistant: Consistency issues are the most common intermediate espresso problem. The variables that affect extraction are interconnected and small changes compound. The usual suspects: dose weight varying day to day, grind size not being adjusted for beans aging or humidity changes, water temperature fluctuations, distribution and tamping inconsistency, and using beans at different ages in their roast cycle. What's your current workflow, and have you been weighing inputs and outputs?

### Human: I weigh the dose at 18g consistently. I grind right before pulling, distribute with a WDT tool, tamp until it stops moving. Output I aim for around 36g in about 25-30 seconds. I use a mid-range setup - Breville Barista Express grinder replaced with a Eureka Mignon.

### Abstract: Your process is solid - separate grinder from the Breville is a good upgrade, WDT and weighing shows you're past beginner. With that workflow, the variability is likely coming from: bean age - espresso tastes different at 5 days post-roast versus 21 days. As beans degas and age, they extract differently. Grind size that was perfect at day 7 might be too coarse by day 14. Are you tracking when you opened the bag and adjusting grind as beans age? Environmental factors - humidity and temperature affect grind size needs. Some people adjust grind daily; at minimum, adjust when there's a significant weather change. Extraction consistency - is your 25-30 second range because you're targeting that, or because it varies within that window naturally? A shot at 25 seconds extracts differently than 30 seconds. Try tightening to 27-29 second target. Puck prep - even with WDT and consistent tamp, if distribution before WDT isn't consistent, you can have channeling. Watch for signs: blonde spots starting early, spurts from the portafilter. What beans are you using - same roaster consistently, different origins, different ages?

### Human: I buy from a local roaster but I tend to grab whatever looks interesting - different origins, different roast levels. Could that be part of the problem?

### Abstract: That's almost certainly a major factor. Different origins and roast levels require significantly different approaches: roast level - light roasts need finer grind, higher temperature, and often higher ratios (1:2.5-3) to extract properly. Dark roasts need coarser grind, can go with lower ratios (1:1.5-2), and are more forgiving. If you're using the same parameters for both, one or both will be suboptimal. Origin and processing - different beans have different densities and extract differently. A washed Ethiopian and a natural Brazilian are different animals even at the same roast level. For consistency building: pick one bean (or one type - same origin, same roast level) and dial it in completely. Learn what that specific bean should taste like at its best. Use it until you really understand it, then explore. This gives you a baseline. When you change beans, expect to re-dial. Different beans aren't just "adjust grind slightly" - they might need different dose, ratio, and temperature. This is why many home espresso enthusiasts have a "house blend" for consistency and experiment with other beans as a separate exercise.
