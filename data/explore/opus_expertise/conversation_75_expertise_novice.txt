HUMAN: What is virtual reality? I've seen those big headsets but don't really get what they do.

A: Virtual reality (VR) immerses you in a computer-generated 3D world. When you wear a VR headset, the screen fills your vision with this virtual environment. As you move your head, the view changes as if you're really there. It can feel like being transported to another place entirely.

H: What can you do in VR?

A: Many things! Play immersive games where you're inside the action. Experience virtual travel - explore museums, landmarks, or fantasy worlds. Watch movies on a giant virtual screen. Exercise with fitness games. Attend virtual social gatherings. Practice skills like public speaking or surgery in safe simulations.

H: Is it just for gaming?

A: Gaming is biggest, but not exclusively. VR is used for job training, education, therapy (treating phobias, PTSD), architecture visualization, virtual meetings, fitness, and artistic creation. As technology improves, uses keep expanding. It's still evolving beyond entertainment.

H: Does VR make people sick?

A: Some people experience motion sickness, especially at first. It happens when what you see doesn't match what your body feels. Better technology has reduced this. Starting with stationary experiences helps your brain adjust. Most people find it decreases with use. Some are more sensitive than others.

H: How expensive is VR?

A: Entry-level standalone headsets like Meta Quest start around $300-500. High-end systems that connect to gaming PCs can cost $500-1000 plus the PC itself. There are cheaper smartphone-based options, but quality varies. For casual exploration, standalone headsets offer good value.

H: What's the difference between VR and AR?

A: VR completely replaces your view with a virtual world - you can't see the real room around you. AR (Augmented Reality) overlays digital elements onto the real world. Think Pokémon Go on your phone or furniture apps that show how a couch would look in your room. AR adds to reality; VR replaces it.

H: Is VR isolating? I worry about people becoming disconnected.

A: Valid concern! VR can be isolating if used excessively. However, many VR experiences are social - virtual rooms where you interact with other people's avatars. Some find it actually connects them with distant friends and communities. Like any technology, moderation matters.

H: Do I have enough space for VR?

A: It depends on the experience. Some VR games are played standing/sitting in place and need minimal space. Active games where you move around need a few square meters of clear floor. Headsets have safety boundaries to warn you before hitting walls. A small clear space in a living room often works.

H: Should I buy a VR headset?

A: Try before you buy if possible! VR is exciting but not for everyone. Some people love it; others feel indifferent or uncomfortable. Many stores have demos, or maybe a friend has one to try. If gaming, fitness games, or virtual experiences appeal to you, it can be worthwhile.
