HUMAN: I'm building a background job system. Should I use Redis with Bull, RabbitMQ, or something else?

A: Depends on your needs. Redis with Bull/BullMQ is simplest for Node.js - easy setup, good dashboard, handles most cases. RabbitMQ offers more sophisticated routing and delivery guarantees. AWS SQS is managed and integrates well with AWS. What are your reliability requirements?

H: We need jobs to be processed at least once, even if servers restart. Jobs include sending emails and processing uploads.

A: All three handle persistence and at-least-once delivery. Redis with Bull: set Redis persistence (RDB or AOF), Bull handles job retries and state. RabbitMQ: durable queues and persistent messages. SQS: fully managed, durable by design. For your use case, Bull is probably simplest.

H: What happens if a job fails? How do I handle retries?

A: Bull has built-in retry with configurable backoff (linear, exponential). Set max attempts per job type. Failed jobs after max retries go to a "failed" state for manual review. Add failure logging with context. For critical jobs, consider dead letter queues.

H: Should each job type be its own queue?

A: Separate queues give you independent scaling and prioritization. Email queue might process faster than video-processing queue. You can have different concurrency limits per queue. Start with separate queues for different job types - it's easier than splitting later.

H: How do I monitor jobs and investigate failures?

A: Bull has Bull Board (web dashboard) showing queues, active/failed jobs, and job details. Log job starts, completions, and failures with job metadata. For production, consider alerting on failed job rate. Store enough context in job data to debug without accessing external systems.

H: What about scheduled jobs? We need nightly report generation.

A: Bull supports scheduled/delayed jobs with cron syntax or delay in milliseconds. For reliability, store schedule definitions in database and have a process that enqueues jobs at the right times. This prevents schedule loss if the scheduler restarts.
