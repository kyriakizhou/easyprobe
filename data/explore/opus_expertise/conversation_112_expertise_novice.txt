HUMAN: What's a QR code? I see those square patterns everywhere now.

A: A QR code is a special barcode that stores information in a square pattern of black and white dots. When you scan it with your phone's camera, it can open a website, show text, connect to WiFi, or do other things. "QR" stands for "Quick Response."

H: How do I scan one?

A: Most modern phones can scan QR codes just by pointing the regular camera at them. Open your camera app, aim at the QR code, and a link or notification should appear. If that doesn't work, you can download a free QR code scanner app.

H: Are they safe to scan?

A: Mostly yes, but use caution with random QR codes in public places. Scammers sometimes put fake QR codes over legitimate ones, leading to phishing websites. Before entering any information after scanning, check that the website looks legitimate and has the correct address.

H: Why do restaurants use them for menus?

A: It started heavily during COVID-19 to reduce touching shared menus. Now restaurants like them because they can update menus instantly without reprinting, save money on printing costs, and some think they're more modern. Though many people still prefer paper menus!

H: Can I make my own QR code?

A: Yes! There are free websites where you type in a link or text, and they generate a QR code for you to download. People use them for business cards, event invitations, or sharing WiFi passwords with guests.

H: What other information can they store?

A: Lots! Website links are most common, but they can store contact information, WiFi network details, calendar events, phone numbers, plain text messages, or app download links. They can hold about 3,000 characters of information in that small square.
