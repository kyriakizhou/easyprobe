HUMAN: What's an emoji and why are there so many?

A: Emojis are small pictures you can include in text messages, emails, and social media. They started as simple smiley faces in Japan and evolved into thousands of images representing emotions, objects, activities, and more. They add visual expression to plain text.

H: Where do new emojis come from?

A: There's actually an organization called the Unicode Consortium that decides which emojis become official. Anyone can propose a new emoji, but it has to meet certain criteria and be approved. New emojis are released annually and then phone/computer makers create their versions.

H: Why do emojis look different on iPhone versus Android?

A: Each company designs their own emoji artwork following Unicode's descriptions. Apple, Google, Samsung, and others all interpret them slightly differently. The same emoji code might be a cartoonish style on one device and more realistic on another.

H: Can emojis cause misunderstandings?

A: Yes! The same emoji can mean different things to different generations or cultures. The slightly smiling face 🙂 seems positive to some but passive-aggressive to others. The skull 💀 means death to some but "I'm dying laughing" to younger users. Context and knowing your audience matters.

H: How do I find the emoji I want?

A: On phones, the emoji keyboard usually has categories like smileys, animals, food, etc. Most also have a search feature - just type a word like "happy" or "dog" to find related emojis. On computers, there are keyboard shortcuts to open emoji pickers.

H: Are there too many emojis now?

A: Some people think so! There are over 3,000 emojis now. But they've expanded to be more inclusive with different skin tones, family combinations, and representations of disabilities. What seems unnecessary to one person might be meaningful representation to another.
