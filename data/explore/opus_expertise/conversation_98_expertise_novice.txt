HUMAN: What's the difference between WiFi and internet?

A: They're related but different! Internet is the global network connecting computers worldwide. WiFi is a wireless way to connect your device to a router. The router connects to the internet through a cable. WiFi is just the wireless "last mile" from router to device - not the internet itself.

H: So I can have WiFi without internet?

A: Yes! If your internet service goes down, your router might still broadcast WiFi - you can connect but can't access the web. You'd see the WiFi symbol on your phone but pages wouldn't load. WiFi is the local connection; internet is what gives it access to the world.

H: What's a router do exactly?

A: A router creates your home network and connects it to the internet. It takes one internet connection and shares it with all your devices - phones, laptops, TVs, smart devices. It also provides security between your home network and the public internet.

H: What's my modem then?

A: The modem translates signals from your internet service provider (cable, fiber, DSL) into data your router can use. Some homes have separate modem and router; many have combined devices. The modem is the gateway to your internet service; the router manages your home network.

H: Why is my WiFi sometimes slow when I'm paying for fast internet?

A: Several reasons! WiFi speed depends on distance from router, walls blocking signal, interference from other devices, and how many devices are connected. You might have fast internet but slow WiFi if the wireless part is the bottleneck. Wired connections (ethernet) are more reliably fast.

H: How can I improve my WiFi?

A: Move your router to a central location. Elevate it - don't put it on the floor. Keep it away from thick walls and metal objects. Consider a mesh WiFi system for larger homes. Reduce interference from other electronics. Connect important devices with ethernet cables if possible.

H: What's 2.4GHz versus 5GHz WiFi?

A: Two different frequencies your router might broadcast. 2.4GHz reaches farther and penetrates walls better but is slower and more crowded. 5GHz is faster but doesn't travel as far. Many routers broadcast both; connect to 5GHz when close by, 2.4GHz when far away.

H: This explains why my WiFi works but sometimes I can't use the internet!

A: Exactly! When WiFi works but internet doesn't, the issue is likely your internet service, modem, or router's connection to the internet - not the WiFi itself. Restarting your modem and router often fixes this by re-establishing the internet connection.

H: I'll remember: WiFi is local, internet is global!

A: Perfect summary! WiFi is your personal wireless network at home. Internet is the worldwide network your home connects to. Understanding this helps troubleshoot problems - is it a WiFi issue or an internet issue? They need different solutions.
