HUMAN: We're implementing a vector similarity search index. What are the trade-offs between different ANN algorithms?

A: ANN (Approximate Nearest Neighbor) algorithms trade accuracy for speed. Main categories: tree-based (Annoy, KD-trees), hash-based (LSH), graph-based (HNSW, NSW), and quantization-based (IVF, PQ). HNSW dominates for many use cases - good recall at high speed. Choice depends on: dataset size, dimensionality, and update frequency.

H: How does HNSW (Hierarchical Navigable Small World) work?

A: Multi-layer graph. Higher layers have fewer nodes, longer-range connections. Search: start at top layer, greedily navigate to nearest neighbors, descend to next layer, repeat. Construction: insert nodes layer by layer, connect to nearest existing nodes. Tunable: M (connections per node), ef (search width).

H: What about quantization approaches like PQ?

A: Product Quantization: split vector into subvectors, quantize each independently with smaller codebook. Storage: ~4-8 bytes per vector instead of hundreds. Search: approximate distances using codebook lookups. Often combined with IVF: first narrow to candidate clusters, then PQ distance within clusters.

H: How do we handle updates and deletions efficiently?

A: HNSW: deletes create tombstones, periodic rebuild to reclaim. Inserts integrate naturally but may degrade quality over time. IVF: updates require cluster reassignment. Trade-off: rebuild frequency vs query quality degradation. Some systems: separate mutable and immutable segments, merge periodically.

H: What metrics matter for evaluating these indexes?

A: Recall@K: fraction of true K-nearest found. QPS: queries per second at given recall target. Build time and index size. Also: memory usage, update latency, and recall stability over time with updates. Test with your actual query distribution - synthetic benchmarks may not reflect production.
