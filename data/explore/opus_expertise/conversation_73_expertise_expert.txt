HUMAN: We're implementing a distributed lock service. What are the safety guarantees we need to provide?

A: Core guarantees: mutual exclusion (only one holder), liveness (lock eventually available if holder dies), and fencing (prevent stale holders from acting). Implementation: consensus-based (ZooKeeper, etcd) or single-node with replication. Fencing tokens are critical for correctness.

H: How do fencing tokens work?

A: Lock acquisition returns monotonically increasing token. Lock holder includes token in all operations. Resources reject operations with token older than last seen. Example: storage accepts writes only if token > stored token. This prevents delayed operations from stale lock holders.

H: What happens during network partitions?

A: Lock holder may be partitioned from lock service but still operating. Without fencing, holder thinks it has lock but service has granted to another. Fencing tokens solve this. Also: lock TTL - if holder can't heartbeat, lock expires. Holder must verify lock validity before critical operations.

H: How do we implement leader election on top of distributed locks?

A: Acquire lock with unique ID, hold while leader. Heartbeat to extend lease. On failure to extend, step down. Other candidates wait on lock. Important: leader must stop acting before lock expires. Use shorter operation timeout than lock TTL. Handle: split brain during network issues.

H: What's the relationship between locks and consensus?

A: Correct distributed locks require consensus (or equivalent). Single-node lock service: consensus among clients that it's the authority. Replicated lock service: internal consensus for lock state. Weak locks (advisory, no fencing): don't require consensus but only for optimization, not correctness.
