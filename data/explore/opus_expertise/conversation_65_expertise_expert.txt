HUMAN: We're implementing a consensus algorithm for our distributed system. Why might we choose Raft over Paxos?

A: Raft was designed for understandability. Single leader simplifies reasoning. Clear separation of concerns: leader election, log replication, safety. Paxos: more flexible but harder to implement correctly, especially Multi-Paxos for state machines. Raft has stronger leader, which simplifies but can limit throughput.

H: What are Raft's performance limitations?

A: Single leader handles all writes - bottleneck. All log entries go through leader - network fan-out from one node. Leader failure triggers election timeout - availability blip. Solutions: Multi-Raft (shard data, separate Raft groups), pipelining, batching. Read scaling: follower reads with lease.

H: How do follower reads work without violating consistency?

A: Options: read from leader (always consistent but doesn't scale), read from follower with leader confirmation, or lease-based reads. Lease: leader grants time-bounded lease, follower can serve reads during lease. If leader can't reach follower, won't grant new lease, preventing stale reads.

H: What's the role of pre-vote in Raft?

A: Pre-vote prevents disruptive elections from partitioned nodes. Before incrementing term and requesting votes, candidate first checks if it would win. Partitioned node's pre-votes rejected (others have more recent leader), so it doesn't disrupt the cluster by incrementing terms.

H: How do we handle configuration changes safely?

A: Single-server changes are simpler and safe. For joint consensus (multiple changes): two-phase, cluster operates in joint configuration temporarily. Raft paper's joint consensus ensures no two leaders in different configurations. In practice, single-server changes with careful sequencing are often sufficient.
