HUMAN: We're implementing a connection pool for database connections. What are the design considerations for high-concurrency scenarios?

A: Key decisions: pool size limits (min/max), connection validation, timeout handling, and connection lifecycle. For high concurrency: separate pools per database/user, warm up connections at startup, and efficient waiting (avoid thundering herd). Monitor: wait time, active count, validation failures.

H: How do we size the pool correctly?

A: Formula consideration: connections = (core_count * 2) + effective_spindle_count is a starting point for databases. But: measure under load. Too small = queuing delays. Too large = database overload, context switching. Consider: different pools for different query types (fast/slow).

H: What about connection validation - how often should we check?

A: Options: validate on borrow, validate on return, or background validation. On borrow: adds latency. On return: may not catch time-based disconnects. Background: periodic health check, evict bad connections. Best: background validation plus timeout-based eviction. Avoid expensive validation (full query) in hot path.

H: How do we handle connection leaks?

A: Detect: connection held longer than threshold without activity. Response: log stack trace of acquirer, optionally force-close after longer timeout. Prevention: try-with-resources patterns, wrapper that tracks acquisition time. In tests: assert no connections held at end.

H: What's the right behavior when pool is exhausted?

A: Options: block and wait (with timeout), fail fast, or create temporary connection (over soft limit). Block with timeout is common - surface issues to callers. Queue fairly (FIFO). Monitor queue length - persistent queuing indicates undersized pool or slow queries.
