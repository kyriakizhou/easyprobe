HUMAN: What's an ebook? Is it exactly like a regular book?

A: An ebook is a digital book you read on a screen - phone, tablet, e-reader, or computer. The text is the same as the printed version, but you can adjust font size, search for words, and carry thousands of books in one device. Some people love the convenience; others prefer paper.

H: Do I need a special device to read ebooks?

A: Not necessarily! You can read ebooks on phones and tablets using apps like Kindle or Apple Books. Dedicated e-readers like Kindle Paperwhite or Kobo have special screens that look like paper and are easier on the eyes for long reading. But any device with a screen works.

H: Where do I get ebooks?

A: Amazon's Kindle Store is the largest. Apple Books works on Apple devices. Libraries offer free ebook borrowing through apps like Libby. There are also smaller stores and free ebook sites for public domain books. Many are cheaper than print versions.

H: Can I share ebooks like regular books?

A: It's more limited due to digital rights management (DRM). Some platforms allow lending to friends for limited periods. You can't just hand it over like a physical book. Libraries solve this by offering ebook lending, though with waitlists for popular titles.

H: What about highlighting and taking notes?

A: Digital books actually excel here! You can highlight passages, take notes, and they're all searchable and organized. You can even export your annotations. No worrying about marking up a beautiful book - it's all digital.

H: Do ebooks cost less than regular books?

A: Usually, but not always! Popular new releases might cost nearly the same. Older books and classics are often much cheaper or free. You save on storage space at home and never have to worry about damaging the physical book.
