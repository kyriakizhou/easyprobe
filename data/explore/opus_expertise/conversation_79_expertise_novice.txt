HUMAN: What's the difference between JPG, PNG, and PDF files? When should I use each?

A: They serve different purposes! JPG (or JPEG) is for photos - it compresses images small but loses some quality. PNG preserves quality and supports transparency - good for graphics and logos. PDF is for documents meant to look the same on any device - like a digital printout. Use each for what it's designed for.

H: When would I use a JPG?

A: Photographs! Camera photos are typically JPGs. When you need to share a photo online or email it, JPG keeps files small enough to send while looking good. Social media, website photos, and casual sharing all work well as JPG. The compression makes files manageable.

H: When is PNG better?

A: Graphics with solid colors, logos, screenshots, and anything needing transparency. When you need a logo on a colored background, PNG's transparency lets the background show through. JPG can't do this - transparent areas would become white. PNG files are larger but preserve quality exactly.

H: What about PDF?

A: Documents! When you want to send something that should look exactly as you created it - resumes, contracts, reports, forms. PDFs preserve fonts, formatting, and layout regardless of what computer or phone opens them. Unlike Word documents, the formatting won't shift based on the viewer's software.

H: Can I convert between these formats?

A: Generally yes, but some conversions lose information. JPG to PNG works but won't add transparency. PNG to JPG loses transparency. PDF to JPG/PNG converts pages to images - you lose text selectability. The best practice is to save in the right format from the start when possible.

H: Why are there so many file types?

A: Different needs! Photography needs efficient compression (JPG). Graphics need perfect quality and transparency (PNG). Documents need consistent appearance everywhere (PDF). Web needs fast loading (various optimized formats). Each format solves specific problems better than a one-size-fits-all approach could.

H: What about GIF?

A: GIFs support animation - those short looping clips you see online. They're also used for simple graphics with few colors. Quality is limited to 256 colors, so photos look bad as GIFs. For non-animated images, PNG or JPG are usually better. For animation, GIF remains popular.

H: How do I know what format a file is?

A: Look at the file extension - the letters after the period in the filename. Photo.jpg is a JPEG. Logo.png is a PNG. Document.pdf is a PDF. Your computer/phone often hides extensions by default but you can reveal them in file settings.

H: This helps! I was always randomly picking formats.

A: Now you can choose intentionally! Photos → JPG. Graphics/logos → PNG. Documents → PDF. When unsure, think about the content and how it will be used. The right format makes files work better and look sharper.
