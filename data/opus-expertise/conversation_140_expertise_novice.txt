HUMAN: What's airplane mode? Do I really need it on planes?

A: Airplane mode is a setting that turns off your phone's wireless communications - cellular, WiFi, and Bluetooth. The name comes from airline requirements, but it's useful anytime you want to disconnect completely. A single toggle shuts off all radio signals your phone emits.

H: Why do planes care about my phone's signal?

A: Historically, there were concerns that phone signals might interfere with aircraft navigation and communication equipment. While modern studies suggest the risk is minimal, airlines still require airplane mode as a precaution. It's also about reducing passenger phone noise during flights.

H: Does airplane mode actually save battery?

A: Yes, significantly! Searching for cell signals is one of the biggest battery drains, especially in areas with weak coverage. Airplane mode stops this completely. It's useful for conserving battery when you don't need to be connected.

H: Can I use WiFi while in airplane mode?

A: Yes! You can turn airplane mode on, then manually re-enable WiFi. Many planes now offer WiFi, and this is exactly how you'd use it - airplane mode on for cellular, WiFi on for internet. Same goes for Bluetooth headphones.

H: When else would I use airplane mode?

A: Anytime you want to disconnect - focus time with no notifications, saving battery, or avoiding roaming charges when traveling internationally. Some people turn it on overnight. It's basically a "leave me alone" mode for your phone's wireless features.

H: Does airplane mode stop my alarms from working?

A: No! Alarms, music stored on your phone, and offline apps work fine in airplane mode. You just can't make calls, send texts, or use internet. It's only the wireless connections that get turned off, not all phone functions.
