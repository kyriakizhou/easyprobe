HUMAN: What's a meme? I see funny pictures called memes online.

A: A meme is a piece of media - usually an image, video, or phrase - that spreads rapidly online, often with variations. Think of inside jokes that millions of people share. They're typically humorous, referencing pop culture or current events. The same image template gets reused with different captions.

H: Where did the word "meme" come from?

A: Scientist Richard Dawkins coined it in 1976 to describe how ideas spread through culture like genes spread through biology. The internet adopted it to describe these viral bits of content. It's pronounced "meem," rhyming with "dream."

H: How do memes spread?

A: People share them on social media, messaging apps, and forums. When something is funny and relatable, people want to share it with friends. Some memes stay in small communities; others become globally recognized. The most viral ones get millions of shares.

H: Why do people find the same pictures funny?

A: Memes work because they capture shared experiences or reactions. A meme about Mondays being terrible resonates with workers everywhere. Recognition of the format also adds to the humor - once you know the meme, each new variation builds on that knowledge.

H: Can anyone make a meme?

A: Absolutely! There are websites and apps where you add text to popular image templates. Many successful memes are made by regular people with a funny idea. Some are made quickly in response to current events. The barrier to entry is very low.

H: Why do memes seem to change so fast?

A: Internet culture moves quickly! Memes burn bright but fast - overuse makes them feel stale. Platforms constantly surface new content, so yesterday's hilarious meme is today's outdated reference. Some memes become "classics" though, and are understood for years.
