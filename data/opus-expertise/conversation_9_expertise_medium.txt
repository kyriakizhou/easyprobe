HUMAN: I've been running consistently for two years, averaging about 30 miles per week. I want to improve my half marathon time from 1:50 to under 1:40. How should I approach my training differently?

A: That's an achievable goal with smart training! A 10-minute improvement requires deliberate work. First question: what does your weekly training look like currently? What mix of easy runs, tempo work, and speedwork are you doing?

H: Mostly easy runs, maybe one tempo run per week. I haven't done much interval work.

A: That's your opportunity. The 80/20 rule suggests 80% of mileage at easy pace, 20% at higher intensities - but that 20% needs variety. Add a weekly interval session: something like 6x1000m at slightly faster than goal pace with recovery jogs. Your tempo runs are good - make sure they're at threshold pace, comfortably hard but sustainable.

H: How do I know if my easy runs are actually easy enough?

A: Most runners run their easy days too fast. You should be able to hold a full conversation - not just a few words. Check heart rate if you use a monitor - easy runs should be zone 2, typically 60-70% of max HR. If you're gasping or need recovery after easy runs, you're going too hard and compromising your hard workout quality.

H: Should I increase my weekly mileage?

A: More mileage builds the aerobic base that supports faster racing. Going from 30 to 40-45 mpw over several months would help. Increase gradually - add miles to easy days first, not to workouts. Follow the 10% rule loosely and include down weeks every 3-4 weeks where you reduce volume by 25-30%.

H: What about strength training? I haven't done any.

A: Highly recommended for injury prevention and running economy. Focus on lower body - squats, lunges, deadlifts, single-leg exercises - and core stability. Two sessions per week is sufficient. You don't need to lift heavy; moderate resistance with good form builds the strength that helps you maintain form late in races.

H: What should my race strategy be for the half?

A: For a 1:40 goal, that's about 7:38 pace. Most runners do better with slight negative splits - start conservatively, pick up in the second half. First few miles should feel easy. Miles 7-10, settle into goal pace. Miles 11-13.1, push. Running even or negative splits prevents the suffering of starting too fast and dying.

H: How long before the race should I start a specific training block?

A: A 12-16 week half marathon plan works well. First few weeks build base, middle weeks have peak volume and workout intensity, final 2-3 weeks taper by reducing volume while maintaining some intensity. Popular plans from Pfitzinger, Hansons, or Higdon can structure this for you.

H: This gives me a clear direction. I'll add intervals and gradually build mileage.

A: Good plan. Trust the process and be patient - fitness builds over months. With consistent training, sub-1:40 is definitely within reach. Good luck!
