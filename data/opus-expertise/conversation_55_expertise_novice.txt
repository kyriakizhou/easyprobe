HUMAN: What is email? I mean, I use it, but how does it actually work?

A: Email is electronic mail - messages sent over the internet from one person to another. When you send an email, it travels from your email provider (like Gmail) through the internet to the recipient's email provider, where it waits in their inbox until they read it. It's like postal mail but instant and digital.

H: Where do my emails actually live?

A: They're stored on servers - powerful computers owned by your email provider. When you check Gmail, your phone or computer connects to Google's servers and downloads or displays your messages. This is why you can access the same email from your phone, computer, or any browser - it's all coming from the same place.

H: What's the difference between To, CC, and BCC?

A: "To" is the main recipient. "CC" (carbon copy) sends a copy to others you want to keep informed - everyone can see who's CC'd. "BCC" (blind carbon copy) sends a secret copy - other recipients don't know the BCC person got the email. Use BCC for privacy when emailing groups who shouldn't see each other's addresses.

H: Why is spam called spam?

A: It's named after a Monty Python sketch where the word "spam" is repeated annoyingly over and over! Early unwanted internet messages were compared to this. Now "spam" means any unsolicited bulk email - advertisements, scams, and junk that clogs your inbox. Filters catch most of it automatically.

H: How does my email provider decide what's spam?

A: Spam filters use many signals: known spam senders, suspicious links, trigger words like "free money," sending patterns, and what you've previously marked as spam. They're not perfect - legitimate mail sometimes goes to spam, and some spam gets through. Check your spam folder occasionally.

H: Is email private?

A: Somewhat, but not completely. Your email provider can technically read your messages. Without encryption, messages could theoretically be intercepted in transit. For most everyday use, email is private enough. For truly sensitive information, use encrypted email services or other secure messaging apps.

H: What's a good email etiquette?

A: Use clear subject lines so people know what the email is about. Be concise - get to the point. Reply promptly to important messages. Use "Reply All" carefully - not everyone needs every response. Don't use all caps (it feels like shouting). Proofread before sending. Include a signature with your name.

H: Should I delete old emails or keep them?

A: Personal preference! Some people archive everything as a searchable record. Others regularly delete to reduce clutter. Important emails - receipts, confirmations, legal or financial matters - are worth keeping. Storage is cheap now, so keeping emails "just in case" is reasonable. Organize with folders or labels.

H: I've used email for years but never thought about how it works. Thanks!

A: That's true for many technologies we use daily! Understanding the basics helps you use email more effectively and securely. It's a simple concept - electronic messages between people - that an elaborate system of servers and protocols makes possible.
