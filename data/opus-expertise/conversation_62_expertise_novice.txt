HUMAN: I keep seeing QR codes everywhere. What are they and how do they work?

A: QR codes are those square patterns of black and white blocks you scan with your phone camera. They store information - usually a web link, but could be text, contact info, or other data. When you scan one, your phone reads the pattern and takes you to whatever it links to.

H: How does the phone read those squiggly patterns?

A: The pattern is actually data encoded in a specific format! The three big squares in the corners help the camera orient the code. The smaller patterns contain the actual information in binary - black squares and white squares representing 1s and 0s. Your camera decodes this pattern into text or a URL.

H: Are QR codes safe to scan?

A: Mostly, but be cautious! A QR code can lead to any website, including malicious ones. Don't scan codes from untrusted sources. Be suspicious of codes that seem randomly placed or cover existing codes. Your phone usually shows the URL before opening it - check that it looks legitimate.

H: Can I make my own QR codes?

A: Yes, easily! Many free websites let you enter text or a link and generate a QR code instantly. You could create one linking to your website, social media profile, WiFi password, or any information you want to share. Just search "QR code generator" and you'll find many options.

H: Why are restaurants using them for menus?

A: It started during COVID to reduce physical contact. Now many keep them because they're convenient - no printing costs, menus can be updated instantly, and customers always see current options and prices. Some people like the convenience; others miss paper menus.

H: What happens if part of a QR code is damaged?

A: QR codes have built-in error correction! They can still work even if up to 30% is damaged or covered. That's why you sometimes see logos placed in the center of QR codes - there's redundancy in the data. However, too much damage will make them unreadable.

H: What's the difference between QR codes and barcodes?

A: Barcodes are the lines on products - they store small amounts of information (usually just a product ID number). QR codes store much more data in two dimensions instead of just one. QR codes can contain URLs, paragraphs of text, or other complex information that wouldn't fit in a simple barcode.

H: How do I scan them with my phone?

A: On most modern phones, just open the camera and point at the code. You should see a prompt or notification to open the link. No special app needed! iPhones and recent Androids have this built in. If yours doesn't work, you can download a QR scanner app from your app store.

H: I was always curious about these but never knew how they worked!

A: They're simple but clever! Essentially pictures that encode data in a format cameras can read. Now that you understand them, you'll notice them everywhere - on products, signs, business cards, tickets. They've become a ubiquitous way to bridge physical and digital information.
