HUMAN: What's a hotspot? Someone asked if I could share my hotspot.

A: A hotspot turns your phone into a mini WiFi router. It shares your cellular data connection with other devices - like your laptop or a friend's phone. When someone asks to use your hotspot, they want to connect to the internet using your phone's data.

H: How do I turn on my hotspot?

A: On iPhones, go to Settings > Personal Hotspot. On Android, it's usually Settings > Network > Hotspot (varies by phone). Turn it on, and you'll see a password others need to connect. Your phone then broadcasts a WiFi network others can join.

H: Does it cost extra money?

A: It depends on your phone plan. Some plans include hotspot data, others charge extra, and some limit how much hotspot data you can use. Check with your carrier. Either way, hotspot uses your cellular data, so heavy use can quickly consume your monthly allowance.

H: Will using hotspot drain my battery?

A: Yes, significantly! Broadcasting a WiFi signal and processing data for connected devices uses lots of battery. If you need to use hotspot for a while, keep your phone plugged in. It's not great for extended use without power.

H: How many devices can connect to my hotspot?

A: Most phones allow 5-10 devices, but performance decreases with more connections since they all share your cellular bandwidth. One or two devices work best. Your phone settings might show a maximum connection limit.

H: Is it safe to share my hotspot with strangers?

A: Use your password and only share it with people you trust. Anyone connected uses your data and could potentially slow your connection. For security, you can change your hotspot password after someone uses it. Never leave an unsecured hotspot open to the public.
