HUMAN: We're moving from a monolith to microservices. What are the biggest pitfalls to avoid?

A: The biggest mistake is doing it prematurely or breaking apart too aggressively. Start by identifying natural boundaries in your monolith - distinct domains that change independently. Extract one service at a time, prove it works, then continue. Don't aim for dozens of services immediately.

H: We've identified user management and billing as candidates. They're pretty independent.

A: Good choices - they have clear boundaries and different change frequencies. Key decisions: how will they communicate (REST, gRPC, events), how will you handle distributed transactions (spoiler: avoid them), and where will shared data live?

H: Our monolith has a lot of direct database joins between user and billing tables.

A: That's common and problematic for separation. Options: duplicate some data (billing stores its own user info copy, synced via events), API calls between services (introduces latency and coupling), or accept some shared database tables during transition. The pure approach is event-driven synchronization.

H: Event-driven sounds complex. What does that look like?

A: User service publishes events: UserCreated, UserUpdated, UserDeleted. Billing service subscribes and maintains its own read model of user data. Use a message broker like RabbitMQ, Kafka, or managed alternatives. It's eventually consistent but highly decoupled.

H: What about deployment and operations? We currently deploy the whole thing at once.

A: Each service needs its own CI/CD pipeline, deployment, and monitoring. This is significant operational overhead. Consider container orchestration (Kubernetes) but evaluate managed options first. Centralized logging and distributed tracing (Jaeger, Zipkin) become essential.

H: This sounds like a lot of work for two services.

A: It is! That's why the advice is "don't do microservices unless you need them." The benefits of independent scaling and deployment come with significant operational costs. If your team is small, the modular monolith pattern gives many benefits without the distributed systems complexity.
