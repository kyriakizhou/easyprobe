HUMAN: What's spam? I hear about spam emails and spam calls.

A: Spam is unwanted, unsolicited messages - the digital equivalent of junk mail. Spam emails flood your inbox with ads, scams, or phishing attempts. Spam calls are unwanted phone calls, often robocalls trying to sell you something or trick you. Both are annoying and sometimes dangerous.

H: Why do I get so much spam?

A: Your email or phone number likely got shared somewhere - from signing up for services, data breaches, or sold lists. Spammers send millions of messages because even tiny response rates are profitable. Once you're on a list, it's hard to get off completely.

H: How do I stop spam emails?

A: Use your email's spam filter - mark unwanted emails as spam so it learns what to block. Unsubscribe from legitimate newsletters you don't want. Be careful where you share your email. Some people have a secondary email for signups to keep their main inbox cleaner.

H: Why do spam emails sometimes look like they're from real companies?

A: Scammers deliberately impersonate banks, Amazon, Netflix, and other companies to trick you. They copy logos and formatting to look legitimate. Always check the actual sender's email address (not just the display name) and be suspicious of urgent requests for personal information.

H: What about those calls saying my car warranty expired?

A: Classic phone spam! These are often scam robocalls. Legitimate companies rarely call unexpectedly. Don't press any buttons or engage - just hang up. You can register for Do Not Call lists and use call-blocking features on your phone.

H: Can spam be dangerous?

A: Yes! Some spam contains malware or links to phishing sites that steal your information. Never click links or download attachments from unknown senders. Even "unsubscribe" links in suspicious emails can be traps. When in doubt, delete it.
