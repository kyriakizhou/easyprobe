HUMAN: What does "share" mean on my phone? There are so many share options.

A: The share button (often a square with an arrow, or three connected dots) lets you send content from one app to another or to other people. When you tap it, you see options like texting the link to someone, posting it to social media, saving it to notes, or emailing it. It's a way to move content around.

H: Why are there so many options when I tap share?

A: Your phone shows all the possible destinations - apps that can receive what you're sharing. This includes messaging apps, social media, email, cloud storage, and more. Each installed app that can handle that type of content appears as an option.

H: Can I share anything?

A: You can share most digital content - links, photos, videos, files, contact info, location. What you can share depends on what app you're in and what you're looking at. Some content may have restrictions, like if someone disabled sharing on their social media post.

H: What's the difference between sharing and copying?

A: Copying puts content on a clipboard so you can paste it somewhere else - you're creating a duplicate. Sharing sends content somewhere specific - to a person or app. Copy is for manual pasting; share is for quick sending. Both help move content around.

H: If I share a photo, do they get my original?

A: It depends on how you share. Some methods send the full original file. Others compress it to save space, reducing quality. Messaging apps often reduce photo quality. AirDrop between Apple devices or email usually preserves the original.

H: Is sharing the same as AirDrop?

A: AirDrop is one sharing method specific to Apple devices - it transfers directly between nearby iPhones, iPads, and Macs using Bluetooth and WiFi. It's fast and high-quality. Android has similar features like Nearby Share. Both appear as share options.
