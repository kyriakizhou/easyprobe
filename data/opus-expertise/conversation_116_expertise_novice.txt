HUMAN: What's a notification and why do I get so many?

A: A notification is an alert from an app telling you something happened - a new message, email, like, reminder, or update. They pop up on your screen or appear as badges on app icons. You get many because almost every app wants your attention and they're enabled by default.

H: Can I make them stop?

A: Yes! You can control notifications in your phone's Settings. You can turn them off entirely for specific apps, or customize what types each app can send. You can also mute notifications at certain times using "Do Not Disturb" or "Focus" modes.

H: Why do apps send so many?

A: Apps want you to open them regularly, and notifications are their way of pulling you back in. Some notifications are genuinely useful (like a message from a friend), but many are promotional or trying to increase "engagement." It's worth reviewing which apps really deserve your attention.

H: What's the difference between a push notification and regular notification?

A: A push notification is sent from a server to your device even when you're not using the app - like when someone texts you. Regular notifications might just appear from apps you have open. Most people use the terms interchangeably to mean any alert that pops up.

H: Why do apps ask permission to send notifications?

A: Because of privacy and attention concerns, phones require apps to get your permission before sending notifications. This gives you control from the start. If you said yes once, you can still turn them off later in Settings.

H: How do I find the important notifications among all the noise?

A: Good strategy is to be ruthless about which apps can notify you. Keep it limited to things you truly need - messages from people, calendar reminders, important emails. Turn off notifications for games, shopping apps, and social media you don't need instant alerts for.
