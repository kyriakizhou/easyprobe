HUMAN: What's email spam and how do I stop it?

A: Spam is unwanted bulk email - advertisements, scams, and junk that clutters your inbox. Spammers send millions of emails hoping even tiny percentages respond. While you can't eliminate it entirely, you can reduce spam significantly and keep it out of your main inbox.

H: How did spammers get my email address?

A: Multiple ways! Signing up for websites that sell or leak addresses. Data breaches from companies you registered with. Your address being scraped from public websites. Sometimes spammers just guess common addresses. The longer you've had an email, the more lists you're probably on.

H: Why does my email provider let spam through?

A: They block most of it! For every spam email you see, many more were caught. But spam evolves to evade filters. Some borderline emails are hard to classify. Providers err on the side of letting questionable mail through rather than blocking legitimate emails.

H: How do I report or block spam?

A: Mark it as spam/junk! This teaches your email's filter. On Gmail, use "Report spam." On Outlook, move to Junk. Most email apps have spam buttons. Blocking specific senders helps with repeat offenders. Never click "unsubscribe" on obvious spam - it confirms your address is active.

H: Is spam dangerous?

A: Some is! Phishing spam pretends to be from legitimate companies to steal passwords. Malware spam contains dangerous attachments. Scam spam tries to trick you out of money. Don't click links in suspicious emails, open attachments from unknown senders, or respond with personal information.

H: What's the difference between spam and a subscription I forgot about?

A: Legitimate subscriptions include real unsubscribe links that work, come from recognizable companies you've dealt with, and have your name. True spam often has fake or dangerous unsubscribe links, comes from random addresses, and doesn't know your name. When in doubt, search your email for past interactions with the sender.

H: Should I create a separate email for sign-ups?

A: Smart strategy! Use your main email for important things - work, banking, personal contacts. Create a secondary email for shopping, newsletters, and website registrations. If that address gets too spammy, you can abandon it or heavily filter it.

H: How do I clean up an already-spammed inbox?

A: Use your email's bulk select and delete for obvious spam. Search for common spam words to find patterns. Unsubscribe from legitimate newsletters you don't want. Set up filters to auto-delete repeat spam domains. Consider starting fresh with a new email for important contacts.

H: Thanks - I'm going to start being more careful about where I give my email!

A: That's the best prevention! Be selective about sign-ups, use secondary addresses for less important things, and never post your email publicly. Combined with good spam filtering and marking spam when it arrives, you can keep your inbox manageable.
