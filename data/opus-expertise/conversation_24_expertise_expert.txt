HUMAN: We're building a time-series database on top of PostgreSQL for IoT sensor data. What schema design and partitioning strategy would you recommend for handling billions of data points?

A: PostgreSQL can handle time-series at scale with proper design. Key strategies: time-based partitioning (native or TimescaleDB), appropriate compression, careful indexing, and batch writes. Schema: narrow tables with (timestamp, sensor_id, value) rather than wide tables with many columns.

H: We're expecting 100K inserts/second across 10K sensors. Each reading is timestamp + sensor_id + 5 float values.

A: At 100K/s, you need batch inserts (COPY or multi-value INSERT). Individual INSERTs will bottleneck. Partition by time (daily or weekly based on retention). Consider TimescaleDB extension - it handles partitioning, compression, and has time-series-specific optimizations.

H: What indexing strategy? We query by sensor_id + time range.

A: Primary query pattern dictates index. BRIN index on timestamp is space-efficient for time-ordered data. B-tree on (sensor_id, timestamp) for sensor-specific queries. With partitioning, indexes are per-partition - smaller, faster. Don't over-index; writes slow down.

H: TimescaleDB vs native partitioning vs CitusDB?

A: TimescaleDB: purpose-built for time-series, transparent partitioning, compression, continuous aggregates. Best for single-node time-series. Native partitioning: more control, no extension dependency, but manual management. Citus: horizontal scaling across nodes, good for multi-tenant time-series.

H: How do we handle data retention - deleting data older than 90 days?

A: With partitioning, drop entire partitions - instant, no vacuum needed. TimescaleDB: drop_chunks() function. Much faster than DELETE which creates dead tuples. Schedule partition drops and creations automatically. Keep partition boundaries aligned with retention policy.

H: What about pre-aggregation for dashboards that show hourly/daily rollups?

A: Pre-aggregate into summary tables. TimescaleDB continuous aggregates do this automatically and keep them updated. For manual: scheduled job computes aggregates, stores in separate table. Dashboard queries rollups instead of raw data. Massive query performance improvement.
