HUMAN: What's biometrics? My phone asks about fingerprint and face unlock.

A: Biometrics are physical characteristics unique to you that can verify your identity - fingerprints, face geometry, voice patterns, or iris patterns. Your phone can use these to unlock or authenticate instead of typing passwords. Your fingerprint and face are essentially your password.

H: How does fingerprint unlock work?

A: The phone's sensor captures an image of your fingerprint's unique pattern of ridges and valleys. It converts this to a mathematical representation stored securely. When you touch the sensor later, it compares the new scan to the stored one to verify it's you.

H: Is Face ID safe? Can someone use my photo to unlock?

A: Modern face recognition like Apple's Face ID uses depth sensors and infrared cameras, not just a flat image. It maps your face in 3D, so photos won't work. It even works in the dark and recognizes you with glasses or different hairstyles.

H: What if I injure my finger or my face changes?

A: You can usually register multiple fingerprints. If one is temporarily injured, others work. For face recognition, the system learns minor changes over time. For major changes, you might need to re-register. Passcodes always work as backup.

H: Is biometric data stored somewhere that could be hacked?

A: Your biometric data stays on your device in a secure chip, not uploaded to company servers. Even if someone hacked your phone, they'd get the mathematical representation, not your actual fingerprint image. It's designed with security in mind.

H: Should I use biometrics or stick with passwords?

A: Biometrics are convenient and generally secure for most people. They're faster than typing passwords. However, some people prefer passwords for privacy reasons. You can use both - biometrics for convenience with a password as backup.
