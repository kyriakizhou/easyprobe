HUMAN: What's the difference between JPEG, PNG, and GIF? Why are there so many image types?

A: Each format has different strengths! JPEG is great for photos - it compresses well and looks good. PNG preserves quality better and supports transparency - good for logos and graphics. GIF supports animation and is used for those short looping clips. Different images need different formats.

H: What does "compression" mean for images?

A: Compression makes files smaller by removing data. JPEG uses "lossy" compression - it discards some image details you probably won't notice to make files much smaller. PNG uses "lossless" compression - files are bigger but no quality is lost. That's why photos are JPEG but text screenshots look better as PNG.

H: Why do some images have transparent backgrounds and others don't?

A: That's a format feature! PNG and GIF support transparency - where parts of the image are see-through. JPEG doesn't support this, so transparent areas become white or another color. If you need a logo that can sit cleanly on any background, use PNG.

H: Why are GIFs so short and low quality?

A: GIFs were designed in 1987 with huge limitations. They only support 256 colors, which creates that grainy look. They weren't designed for video but got repurposed for short animations. They're popular despite quality because they autoplay everywhere. Newer formats like WebP are better but less supported.

H: When should I use which format?

A: Photos and realistic images: JPEG. Graphics, logos, screenshots, or anything needing transparency: PNG. Simple animations: GIF. If you're unsure, PNG is the safe choice for quality, though files will be larger. For websites, smaller files (JPEG) load faster.

H: What about images for printing?

A: For printing, file format matters less than resolution. High-resolution TIFF or PNG files are preferred by professional printers. JPEG works fine if the resolution is high enough. The key for print is having enough pixels - 300 per inch at the size you're printing is the standard.

H: I've seen HEIC files on my phone. What's that?

A: HEIC is a newer format Apple uses because it's more efficient - same quality as JPEG in half the file size. This saves storage on your phone. The downside is compatibility - some older programs can't open HEIC files. iPhones can be set to convert to JPEG when sharing if needed.

H: This helps a lot. I never knew why my screenshots looked bad when I saved them as JPEG.

A: That's a great example! Screenshots have text and sharp edges that JPEG compression blurs. PNG keeps those crisp. Knowing when to use which format will make your images look better and your files more manageable. It's a small thing that makes a difference!
