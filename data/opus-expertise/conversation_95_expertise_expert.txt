HUMAN: We're implementing a database replication system. What are the trade-offs between physical and logical replication?

A: Physical replication: replicate WAL bytes, exact page-level copy. Faster, lower overhead, but replicas must be identical version/architecture. Logical replication: replicate operations/row changes. More flexible (different versions, partial replication), higher overhead, schema changes need care.

H: When would we choose logical over physical?

A: Logical for: cross-version replication (upgrades), selective replication (subset of tables), multi-master setups, heterogeneous systems (PostgreSQL to MySQL), or when replicas need different indexes. Physical for: simplest high availability, disaster recovery, read replicas of same version.

H: How does logical replication handle schema changes?

A: Challenge: DDL in logical replication. Options: replicate DDL as operations (complex to parse and apply), require manual schema sync, or pause replication during DDL. Many systems require: apply DDL to replica first, then to primary, to avoid breaking change application.

H: What's the replication lag situation for each approach?

A: Physical: lag = time to ship and apply WAL. Typically very low, bounded by network and I/O. Logical: lag = time to decode changes and apply. Decode overhead, and row-by-row apply can be slower than physical page writes. Large transactions: might cause lag spikes during decode.

H: How do we handle large transactions in replication?

A: Large transaction: millions of rows in one commit. Physical: stream WAL as written, apply in order. Logical: decode entire transaction before sending (memory pressure), or streaming logical replication (send as decoded, apply before commit). Monitor transaction size, consider chunking large operations.
