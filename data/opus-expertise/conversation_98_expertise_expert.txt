HUMAN: We're implementing a scheduling algorithm for our container orchestrator. What are the key constraints and objectives?

A: Scheduling objectives: resource utilization (bin packing), spreading for fault tolerance, affinity/anti-affinity constraints, and priority/preemption. Constraints: resource requests/limits (CPU, memory, GPU), node selectors, taints/tolerations, and topology (zones, racks). Multi-objective optimization problem.

H: How do we balance bin packing vs spreading?

A: Pure bin packing: maximize utilization, but failures take out many workloads. Pure spreading: resilient but inefficient. Hybrid: spread across failure domains (zones), bin pack within domains. Also consider: workload type - stateless can pack tighter, stateful needs more spreading.

H: What's the right approach for priority and preemption?

A: Priority classes: critical system pods > production workloads > batch jobs. Preemption: when high-priority pod can't schedule, evict lower-priority pods to make room. Challenges: avoid preemption cascades, ensure lower-priority work eventually runs. Budget: limits on simultaneous evictions.

H: How do we handle resource fragmentation?

A: Fragmentation: total free resources sufficient but scattered across nodes. Solutions: defragmentation (move pods to consolidate free space - disruptive), or proactive: schedule to avoid fragmentation patterns. Also: overcommit with limits > requests, works if not all pods use full limit simultaneously.

H: What scheduling algorithm handles large clusters efficiently?

A: Sequential scheduling: simple but slow. Parallel: multiple schedulers, potential conflicts. Kubernetes approach: informers cache cluster state, scheduler filters then scores nodes. Optimizations: indexed data structures for filters, early termination when good-enough node found. Watch for: scheduler bottleneck at scale.
