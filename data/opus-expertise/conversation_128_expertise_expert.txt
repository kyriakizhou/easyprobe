HUMAN: We're implementing a time-series anomaly detection system. How do we handle seasonality and trend in baseline modeling?

A: Time series decomposition: trend (long-term direction), seasonality (repeating patterns), residual (noise + anomalies). STL decomposition is standard. Anomalies: significant deviations in residual component. Challenge: multiple seasonalities (daily + weekly + monthly).

H: How do we handle multiple seasonal patterns?

A: Fourier terms: model periodic components with sine/cosine. Prophet: multiple seasonality components built-in. MSTL: multiple seasonal decomposition. Alternative: train separate models for different time scales, ensemble results. Consider: which seasonalities matter for your data?

H: What algorithms work well for streaming anomaly detection?

A: Statistical: z-score with rolling statistics, exponential moving average. ML: isolation forest (efficient for high-dimensional), local outlier factor, autoencoder reconstruction error. Online versions: update model incrementally with new data. Trade-off: sensitivity vs false positive rate.

H: How do we handle concept drift in the baseline?

A: Baseline changes over time (new normal). Solutions: sliding window (recent data only), weighted history (recent data weighted more), explicit change detection (detect shifts, rebuild baseline). Monitor: anomaly rate changes (sudden drop might mean baseline drifted).

H: What's the right alerting strategy for anomaly detection?

A: Raw anomaly scores produce too many alerts. Aggregation: alert on persistent anomalies (N of M windows). Severity tiers: based on deviation magnitude. Correlation: multiple related metrics anomalous = higher priority. Feedback loop: track alert accuracy, tune thresholds.
