HUMAN: What's a megabyte versus a gigabyte? Storage numbers confuse me.

A: These are units measuring digital storage, like how liters measure liquid. A byte is tiny - roughly one character of text. A megabyte (MB) is about a million bytes - enough for a few photos. A gigabyte (GB) is about a thousand megabytes - enough for several movies. A terabyte (TB) is a thousand gigabytes.

H: Why does my phone say 128GB?

A: That's your phone's storage capacity - how much total space it has for apps, photos, videos, and files. 128GB can hold thousands of photos, hundreds of apps, and many hours of video. Different phones come with different storage amounts: 64GB, 128GB, 256GB, or more.

H: What's the difference between storage and memory?

A: Storage (measured in GB) is permanent - it's where your files, photos, and apps are saved even when the phone is off. Memory (RAM, also measured in GB) is temporary workspace the phone uses while running apps. More RAM helps run apps smoothly; more storage holds more stuff.

H: Why am I running out of storage?

A: Photos and videos are usually the biggest space users, especially 4K video. Apps also take space, and some grow over time with data. Messages with lots of photos add up too. You can check what's using space in your phone's storage settings.

H: How do I free up storage?

A: Delete old photos and videos (after backing them up), remove unused apps, clear app caches, and delete old messages. Moving photos to cloud storage or a computer frees local space too. Most phones have a built-in storage management tool with suggestions.

H: Is more storage always better?

A: More gives you flexibility, but you might not need the maximum. If you take lots of photos or download movies for offline viewing, get more storage. If you use streaming and cloud storage, less might be fine. You can't usually add storage later, so err on the higher side.
