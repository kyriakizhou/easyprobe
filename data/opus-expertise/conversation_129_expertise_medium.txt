HUMAN: How do I implement proper blue-green or canary deployments?

A: Blue-green: run two identical environments, switch traffic instantly. Canary: gradually shift traffic to new version. Both reduce deployment risk. Require infrastructure support and good rollback procedures.

H: What's the difference between blue-green and canary?

A: Blue-green: all-or-nothing switch between old (blue) and new (green). Instant rollback by switching back. Simple but no gradual validation. Canary: route small percentage (1%, 10%) to new version, increase gradually. Catch problems with limited impact.

H: How do I implement blue-green on AWS?

A: Common approach: two Auto Scaling Groups or ECS services behind a load balancer. Deploy to inactive environment, run smoke tests, switch target group weights in ALB. Keep old environment running for quick rollback. Clean up after stability period.

H: How do I route traffic for canary deployments?

A: ALB weighted target groups: 90% to stable, 10% to canary. Or use service mesh (Istio) for fine-grained routing. CloudFront/CDN can do weighted routing too. Kubernetes: use traffic splitting with Ingress or service mesh.

H: What metrics should I watch during canary rollout?

A: Compare canary to stable: error rates, latency (p50, p95, p99), business metrics (conversion, engagement). Automated analysis can detect anomalies and rollback. Look at both technical health and user experience metrics.

H: How do I handle database migrations with these strategies?

A: Database changes must be backward-compatible. Both versions read/write same database during transition. Add columns nullable, don't remove columns until old version is gone. Expand-contract pattern: expand schema, migrate code, contract schema later.
