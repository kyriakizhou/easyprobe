HUMAN: What's GPS and how does my phone know where I am?

A: GPS stands for Global Positioning System. It's a network of satellites orbiting Earth that constantly broadcast signals. Your phone receives signals from multiple satellites and uses the tiny time differences to calculate your exact position - it's basically triangulation from space!

H: So satellites are watching me?

A: Not exactly! The satellites just broadcast signals constantly - they're not pointing at you specifically. Your phone listens to those signals and does the math locally. The satellites don't know who's receiving their signals. It's more like radio stations broadcasting - they don't know who's listening.

H: Does GPS use my data or cost money?

A: GPS itself is free and doesn't use cellular data - the satellite signals are just there for anyone to receive. However, map apps often download maps using data, which does count against your plan. That's why downloading maps ahead of time is useful for trips.

H: Why is my GPS sometimes wrong?

A: GPS accuracy depends on having clear signals from enough satellites. Buildings, tunnels, dense trees, or canyons can block signals. Being inside weakens signals too. Sometimes you're getting a location estimate from WiFi or cell towers instead of true GPS.

H: What's the difference between GPS and location services?

A: GPS is the satellite system specifically. "Location services" on your phone combines GPS with WiFi networks, cell towers, and other data to figure out where you are. Indoors, where GPS struggles, your phone might use WiFi positioning instead.

H: Should I leave GPS on all the time?

A: It's a personal choice! Having it on means apps can provide location-relevant info, but it uses more battery. You can also control which apps can access location - maybe allow maps but not random games. Most people find a middle ground.
