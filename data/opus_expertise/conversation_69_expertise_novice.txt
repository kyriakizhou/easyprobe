HUMAN: What does "resolution" mean for screens and images? I see numbers like 1080p but don't understand them.

A: Resolution is the number of tiny dots (pixels) that make up an image or screen. More pixels means more detail and sharpness. 1080p means 1920 pixels wide by 1080 pixels tall - about 2 million total pixels. Higher resolution like 4K (3840 x 2160) has four times as many pixels, so images look even sharper.

H: What's the difference between 720p, 1080p, and 4K?

A: Each is progressively sharper. 720p (1280x720) is older HD - looks fine on small screens but fuzzy on big ones. 1080p (1920x1080) is standard Full HD - good for most uses. 4K (3840x2160) is very sharp - noticeable on larger screens but overkill for small ones. The numbers refer to vertical pixels.

H: Does higher resolution always mean better?

A: Not necessarily! You need a screen capable of displaying it, and you need to sit close enough to notice the difference. 4K on a 65" TV looks amazing. 4K on a 5" phone is barely different from 1080p because your eyes can't distinguish pixels that small. File sizes also increase with resolution.

H: Why do some videos look blurry while others are crystal clear?

A: The video's resolution and your internet speed both matter. If a video was recorded in low resolution, it will look blurry even on a great screen. If your internet is slow, streaming services lower quality to prevent buffering. A slow connection might show 480p even on a 4K TV.

H: What resolution should I look for when buying a TV?

A: For TVs 50" and larger, 4K is worth it - the extra detail is noticeable. For smaller TVs or if budget is tight, 1080p still looks good. For computer monitors, 1080p is fine for most work; 1440p or 4K helps if you do detailed visual work or have a larger monitor.

H: What about phone cameras? I see megapixels advertised.

A: Megapixels measure camera resolution. A 12MP camera captures 12 million pixels per photo. More megapixels means bigger photos with more detail when you zoom in. But megapixels aren't everything - lens quality, sensor size, and software processing matter too. 12MP with great processing beats 48MP with bad processing.

H: Does higher resolution use more storage and data?

A: Yes! A 4K video file is about 4x larger than 1080p. Streaming 4K uses more internet data. Higher resolution photos take more storage. This is a trade-off to consider. For most viewing, 1080p offers a good balance of quality and file size.

H: What's the "p" in 1080p anyway?

A: It stands for "progressive scan" - a technical term for how the image is drawn on screen. You might also see "i" (interlaced) on older standards like 1080i. Progressive is generally better. For practical purposes, just focus on the number - higher is sharper.

H: This finally makes sense! I always wondered what those numbers meant.

A: Resolution is simpler than it seems - it's just counting dots! Higher number = more dots = sharper picture. Now you can make informed choices about screens, cameras, and video quality. For most people, 1080p is plenty good, and 4K is nice to have for larger screens.
