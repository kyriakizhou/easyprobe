HUMAN: We're deploying a transformer model for real-time inference. The model is 350M parameters and we need P99 latency under 50ms. Currently we're seeing 80-100ms with TensorRT on A100s. What optimization strategies should we prioritize?

A: At 350M params targeting 50ms P99, you're in challenging territory. First, profile granularly - is latency dominated by tokenization, model inference, or postprocessing? For the model itself: have you implemented KV caching for autoregressive decoding? And what's your current quantization status?

H: KV caching is implemented. We've done INT8 quantization which helped but we're still over target. Profiling shows attention layers are the bottleneck - about 60% of inference time.

A: Attention is often the bottleneck. Are you using FlashAttention? It reduces memory bandwidth requirements through tiled computation, which is typically the limiting factor on modern GPUs. TensorRT doesn't include FlashAttention natively, but TensorRT-LLM does. If you're on raw TensorRT, the integration requires custom plugin work but typically yields 40-60% attention speedup.

H: We haven't implemented FlashAttention yet. Our current batch size is 1 for latency - is there any benefit to batching at low traffic?

A: At batch size 1, GPU utilization is poor - you're paying kernel launch overhead without amortization. Consider continuous batching with speculative decoding if applicable. Even batches of 2-4 can improve throughput significantly with minimal latency impact. Dynamic batching with tight latency deadlines (say, 5ms queue time) can help during traffic spikes while maintaining P99 targets.

H: We've considered distillation to get model size down. What quality tradeoffs should we expect going from 350M to under 100M parameters?

A: That's aggressive compression - expect 5-15% quality regression depending on task complexity. Key success factors: use full teacher outputs as soft labels, not just hard labels; distill layer-by-layer representations, not just final outputs; ensure your distillation dataset covers the full input distribution. Task-specific distillation typically outperforms general capability transfer.

H: What about hybrid quantization strategies? Different precision for different layers?

A: Mixed-precision can help. Attention matrices and output projections often tolerate INT8 well. Embedding layers and final output layers are more sensitive - keep those in FP16. Profile per-layer accuracy degradation on your validation set. Some teams keep the first and last transformer blocks at higher precision while aggressively quantizing middle layers.

H: Our traffic patterns are bursty. How should we handle capacity during spikes without blowing our latency budget?

A: For bursty traffic: implement admission control with request shedding before queues grow too deep, use token bucket rate limiting per client, and consider auto-scaling based on queue depth rather than just CPU/GPU utilization. Pre-warming instances helps if spikes are predictable. Also ensure your load balancer uses least-connections routing to avoid hot-spotting.

H: This gives us a clear optimization roadmap. FlashAttention and continuous batching first.

A: Good prioritization - those typically yield the largest gains. Measure rigorously at each step. And consider whether 50ms P99 is the right target, or if a slightly relaxed SLO with graceful degradation during spikes serves users better.
