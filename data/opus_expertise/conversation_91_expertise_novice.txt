HUMAN: What's the difference between signing in with Google/Facebook versus creating a new account?

A: When you "Sign in with Google/Facebook," you use your existing account to access a new service. It's faster - no new password to remember. Creating a separate account means a new username and password for that specific site. Both have trade-offs in convenience versus privacy.

H: Is signing in with Google convenient or risky?

A: Both! It's convenient because you don't need to remember another password, and you can sign in quickly across devices. The risk is that if your Google account is compromised, attackers could access everything linked to it. Also, it lets Google know which services you use.

H: What happens to my data when I sign in that way?

A: The service gets some information from Google/Facebook - usually your name, email, and profile photo. The exact data shared is shown before you confirm. Some apps request more permissions like access to your contacts. Review what's being shared each time.

H: Should I always create separate accounts instead?

A: Not necessarily! For unimportant services you'll use once, social login is fine - why create another password? For important accounts (banking, email, medical), definitely create separate accounts with unique passwords. Use a password manager to handle the password burden.

H: What if Facebook or Google goes down?

A: If they have outages, you can't log into connected services until they're back. This is rare and usually brief. For critical services, having a separate login provides independence from other companies' technical problems.

H: What if I want to stop using Facebook - what happens to my accounts?

A: You'd need to either keep your Facebook just for login purposes, or connect an email/password to services before deleting Facebook. Many services let you add alternative login methods. It's a process, but possible. This dependency is worth considering before linking everything.

H: Does using these logins give those companies more data about me?

A: Yes, somewhat. They know which services you use and when you access them. This data helps them target ads. If that concerns you, creating separate accounts limits how much any single company knows about your online activity.

H: What about "Sign in with Apple"?

A: Apple offers unique privacy features. You can hide your real email - Apple creates a random forwarding address. Apple also limits tracking between the service and Apple's data. It's generally the most privacy-friendly social login option.

H: So I should think about it instead of just clicking the easy option?

A: Exactly! For quick, unimportant signups, social login is fine. For important services, consider separate accounts. Think about what happens if that central account is compromised. The convenience is real, but so are the trade-offs. Make informed choices based on what the service is.
