HUMAN: I keep getting emails saying my account has been compromised and I need to click a link. Are these real?

A: Most likely not! These are almost always phishing emails - attempts to trick you into giving away your password or personal information. Real companies rarely ask you to click links in emails to verify your account. This is a very common scam.

H: How can I tell if an email is fake?

A: Look for several signs: Check the sender's email address carefully - scammers use addresses that look similar but aren't quite right. Look for spelling errors or awkward grammar. Be suspicious of urgent language like "Act now!" or "Your account will be closed!" Also, hover over links without clicking to see the real destination.

H: What happens if I do click the link?

A: Usually, you'll be taken to a fake website that looks like the real one. If you enter your login info there, the scammers capture it. Sometimes clicking can also download malware. If you've already clicked a suspicious link, change your passwords immediately and scan for viruses.

H: But what if it really is from my bank?

A: If you're unsure, never click the link in the email. Instead, open your browser and go directly to the company's website by typing the address yourself. Or call them using a number you find independently, not one from the email. If there's really a problem, you'll see it when you log in normally.

H: Why do they send these emails?

A: It's a numbers game. Scammers send millions of these emails, and even if only a tiny percentage fall for it, they can steal many accounts. The emails are cheap to send, and stolen accounts can be very profitable - they might access bank accounts, steal identities, or sell the login info.

H: Can my email provider stop these?

A: They try! Most email providers filter obvious phishing into spam. But scammers constantly evolve their tactics to get past filters. That's why you need to stay vigilant even with good spam protection. When in doubt, delete the email.

H: What if I already gave them my information?

A: Act fast! Change the password for that account immediately. If it involves financial information, contact your bank. Enable two-factor authentication if available. Monitor your accounts for suspicious activity. Consider placing a fraud alert on your credit report.

H: This is scary. Thanks for the warning!

A: Stay cautious but not paranoid! Most phishing is easy to spot once you know what to look for. When something seems urgent or too good to be true, that's your signal to slow down and verify.
