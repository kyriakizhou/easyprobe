HUMAN: Everyone keeps telling me I need to save for retirement but I don't even know where to start. I'm 25 and it seems so far away.

A: I understand - retirement feels distant when you're 25! But starting early is actually one of the best financial decisions you can make. Even small amounts saved now can grow significantly over time. Does your job offer any retirement benefits?

H: I think they mentioned a 401k when I started but I didn't really understand it.

A: A 401(k) is a retirement savings account offered through your employer. Money is taken from your paycheck before taxes, which lowers your tax bill now. The best part is that many employers offer "matching" - they'll add extra money when you contribute. It's essentially free money!

H: Wait, they give me free money? How does that work?

A: Yes! For example, your employer might match 50% of what you contribute, up to 6% of your salary. So if you put in 6%, they add another 3%. That match is an instant 50% return on your money - you won't find that anywhere else. Check with your HR department to see what your company offers.

H: How much should I be saving?

A: A common guideline is to save 10-15% of your income for retirement, but don't let that overwhelm you. Start with whatever you can afford - even 1-3% is better than nothing. The most important thing at your age is to just START. You can increase the amount as you earn more.

H: What if I need that money before I retire?

A: Generally, you'll pay penalties if you withdraw from a 401(k) before age 59½, so it's best to think of it as untouchable. For short-term savings, keep a separate emergency fund in a regular savings account. Try to build up 3-6 months of expenses there.

H: This is less scary than I thought. I'll ask HR about my 401k tomorrow.

A: That's great! Even a small step now puts you way ahead. The power of starting young is that your money has decades to grow. Future you will be very thankful you started at 25!
