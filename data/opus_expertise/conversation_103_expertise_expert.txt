HUMAN: We're implementing a distributed key generation system for cryptographic keys. What are the security considerations?

A: Distributed key generation (DKG): generate keys without single party knowing full key. Uses: threshold signatures, distributed encryption. Security: no single point of compromise, verifiable contributions. Protocols: Pedersen DKG, Feldman VSS. Considerations: communication security, participant authentication, and Byzantine fault tolerance.

H: How does threshold key generation work?

A: Shamir's Secret Sharing + Feldman VSS: each participant generates random polynomial, shares points with others, verifiable commitments published. Combined shares give distributed key. t-of-n threshold: any t participants can sign/decrypt, fewer than t learn nothing about key. No dealer needed.

H: What attacks do we need to prevent?

A: Rushing attack: adversary waits to see honest contributions before submitting. Prevention: commitment phase before reveal. Biasing attack: adversary manipulates their contribution to bias final key. Prevention: verifiable randomness. Replay attack: reuse old protocol messages. Prevention: session binding.

H: How do we handle participant failures during DKG?

A: If participant fails during sharing: either restart protocol or use complaint mechanism. Feldman VSS: participants can verify received shares against commitments, publicly complain about invalid shares. Honest participants reconstruct bad share from others' data. Threshold must account for failures.

H: What about key refresh without changing the key?

A: Proactive secret sharing: periodically re-share key with new polynomials. Old shares become useless. Protects against mobile adversary (compromises different participants over time). Implementation: coordinate refresh windows, ensure all participants update simultaneously, handle lagging participants.
