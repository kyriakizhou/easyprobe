HUMAN: What's WiFi calling and should I use it?

A: WiFi calling lets your phone make regular phone calls over a WiFi connection instead of using cellular signal. It's useful when you're somewhere with weak cell coverage but good WiFi - like inside some buildings. Calls sound the same and use your regular phone number.

H: Does it cost extra?

A: For most carriers, WiFi calling is free and included with your plan. The calls count the same as regular calls toward any limits you have. You're essentially just using a different path for the call signal - WiFi instead of cell towers.

H: How do I turn it on?

A: On iPhones, go to Settings > Phone > WiFi Calling. On Android, it's usually in Settings > Connections > WiFi Calling (location varies by phone). Your carrier needs to support it, which most major carriers now do.

H: Is it better quality than regular calls?

A: It can be! WiFi calling often provides clearer audio, especially if you have strong WiFi. However, if your WiFi is weak or congested, call quality might suffer. It's most useful when your regular cell signal is poor but WiFi is good.

H: What happens if I walk away from WiFi during a call?

A: Modern phones handle this pretty well. If you leave WiFi range during a call, the phone can seamlessly switch to cellular if signal is available. You might hear a brief pause, but the call usually continues without disconnecting.

H: Why wouldn't everyone use this?

A: Some people don't know about it! Others have great cell coverage everywhere and don't need it. Some older phones don't support it. But for anyone who struggles with cell signal at home or work, it's worth enabling - there's no downside to having it available.
