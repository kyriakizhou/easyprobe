HUMAN: What's a charger versus a cable? Are they the same?

A: They're different parts! A cable is the cord with connectors at each end - it carries power and data. A charger (or power adapter) is the part that plugs into the wall outlet and converts household electricity to the right voltage for your device. Together they charge your phone, but they're separate components.

H: So I need both to charge my phone?

A: Usually yes! You need the wall adapter and the cable. Some devices come with them separate or together. You can also charge from a computer's USB port using just the cable - the computer provides the power instead of a wall adapter.

H: Why are there different types of cables?

A: Different connectors were developed over time by different companies. Lightning is Apple's cable for iPhones. USB-C is the newer universal standard (now on newer iPhones too). Older Android phones used Micro-USB. The connector shape determines compatibility.

H: Can I use any charger for my phone?

A: You can usually use chargers with the right connector, but there are considerations. Some chargers provide more power for faster charging. Using a very weak charger might be slow. Using incorrect voltage could potentially damage your device, though most modern devices have protection.

H: What about wireless charging?

A: Wireless charging uses a pad you place your phone on instead of a cable. The pad plugs into the wall and transfers power through electromagnetic induction. It's convenient but usually slower than wired charging. Your phone must support it - most newer phones do.

H: Why does my phone charge faster with some chargers?

A: Different chargers provide different amounts of power (measured in watts). A 5W charger is slow; 20W or higher is fast. "Fast charging" requires both a capable charger and a phone that supports it. Using the charger that came with your device usually gives optimal speed.
